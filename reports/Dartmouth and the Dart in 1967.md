# Winding today's Dart back to 1967

Dartmouth in June 1967 had today's street plan and today's reclaimed waterfront, so the game's map is wrong mostly at the water's edge, on the railway land at Kingswear, at the Noss shipyard and in everything that moves. The Embankment was about 6 m narrower and lower, with a roofed wooden ferry pontoon in front of the "station". Kingswear was a staffed British Railways terminus with a manned signal box, an idle goods yard where Darthaven Marina now lies and an older, taller stone lighthouse; Noss was Philip & Son's working shipyard; and Sandquay already had a marina and a 19-bedroom hotel. On the river no paddle steamer was in steam: five motor vessels made up the River Dart Steamboat Co.'s fleet, British Railways ran the passenger ferry with two 1957 launches, the Lower Ferry had two brand-new tugs, and the Higher Ferry was a paddle-driven floating bridge of 1960. Ashore the scene is Western National buses, black number plates no newer than an E suffix, red K6 kiosks, old and new road signs side by side, and a borough of about 5,750 people a few days after the 29 May bank holiday, under a waning moon and (by inference) weak neap tides, with the Six-Day War on the placards from 5 June. The game's 1967 removal list in `era1967.ts` is mostly sound, but it needs four corrections (the "Spar" name match, a wrongly kept Kingswear pontoon, the kept Double Steps pontoon and the wording on Dart Marina) and at least four additions (the Hoodown depot, the training ship *Hindostan*, the clinic on the cinema's site and Greenway Halt). **None of this was checked against a full page:** the session's network policy blocked the primary-source sites and the web-search budget ran out, so every finding rests on search-result summaries and needs a verification pass before it is built or printed.

> **Verification status: read this before using anything below.** The researchers could not open a single source page in full. The session's network policy blocked the primary-source sites (Historic England, British Listed Buildings, Wikipedia, Simplon, kingswearcastle.org, Britain from Above, the National Library of Scotland maps, the Met Office, Dartmouth Museum and others), and the shared web-search budget ran out before the last rounds of queries. Every cited fact is therefore what a search engine's summary reported the named page as saying. The links show where to check, not what was read. No 1960s Ordnance Survey sheet, Admiralty chart, directory, timetable, newspaper or dated colour photograph of Dartmouth or Kingswear was seen. Treat this report as a sourced brief for a verification pass, and do not print dates, names or figures in the game until that pass is done.

**How confidence is marked.** *High* means a statutory listing or official body states it, or two independent sources agree. *Medium* means one reasonable source, or a dated inference from sourced facts. *Low* means a single weak, promotional or forum source, or the researchers' own inference. All three are capped by the caveat above. Sun and moon figures were computed by the researchers (PyEphem 4.2.1) and are reproducible. Statements about the game's own data and code were checked directly in the repository (`public/geo/features.json`, `src/gull/view/era1967.ts`, `src/render`, `src/sim`). Game positions are local metres (east, north) from the Royal Dart Yacht Club.

## Dartmouth kept its plan, but its river edge, pontoon and shop signs have changed

### The reclaimed waterfront was already thirty years old

*Confidence: high. Several sources agree on every structure; only the exact years differ.*

The level ground the game draws along the river existed in full by 1967. The Harbour Commissioners built the **South Embankment in 1882–85**, from the Lower Ferry slip to Vavasour's Slip ([DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/a-brief-history-of-dartmouth/)), and the gap at the railway jetty and the Boat Float was closed about 1889 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_railway_station); [British Listed Buildings](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101218121-the-boat-float-dartmouth)). The North Embankment and Coronation Park were reclaimed from Coombe Mud between 1928 and 1937, and **the park opened in 1937** ([Dartmouth Town Council](https://www.dartmouthtowncouncil.gov.uk/coronation-park/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon); [Devon Historic Coastal and Market Towns Survey](https://dartmouthplan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/J3-Devon-Historic-Coastal-and-Market-Towns-Survey.pdf)). Royal Avenue Gardens already had its 1911 cast-iron bandstand ([Dartmouth Green Partnerships](https://dartmouthgreenpartnerships.org/history/)) and its 1897 fountain ([Parks & Gardens UK](https://www.parksandgardens.org/places/royal-avenue-gardens)). The Newcomen engine house beside the gardens went up in 1963 or 1964 ([Devon Gardens Trust](https://devongardenstrust.org.uk/gardens/royal-avenue-gardens); [Visit South Devon](https://www.visitsouthdevon.co.uk/things-to-do/the-newcomen-engine-p1529933)). The game is therefore right to keep the Embankment, the park, the gardens and the engine house. The Boat Float is unchanged in outline: a trapezoid of water with reclaimed land on three sides and the embankment as its fourth, joined to the river beneath the road ([British Listed Buildings](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101218121-the-boat-float-dartmouth); [Geograph](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/8447939)). The form of its bridge, parapet and railings, and which boats lay in it in 1967, were not found.

### The 1986 flood works replaced the edge the player perches on

*Confidence: high that the pontoon changed; medium for the 6 m figure, whose exact source page is unconfirmed; low for what the old edge looked like.*

What the game must not copy is today's river edge. **The Embankment was widened by about 6 m and raised between 1985 and 1990** to stop flooding at spring tides, according to a search extract that drew on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon) and the [Conservation Area Appraisal](https://dartmouthplan.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Dartmouth_Appraisal_webfinal-2.pdf) without making clear which of the two states it. In those works, in 1986, **the station's original covered wooden pontoon was removed and replaced by a larger open pontoon of concrete and steel** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_railway_station)). The first pontoon, of 1864, was linked to the quay by a bridge ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon)). A 1967 Embankment is therefore a narrower, lower quay without the flood wall, whose centrepiece is a roofed timber pontoon reached by a bridge or gangway from the station building. The station opened with the railway in 1864 as a station without tracks; the present building dates from about 1889–90 and was listed in 1974 as the "former landing stage for ferry from the Kingswear railway terminus" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_railway_station); [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1297055)). In 1967 it was a working British Railways booking office, an inference from BR's operation of the ferry until 1972.

The working waterfront is the weakest part of the town evidence. Dart Harbour today calls the South Embankment the port's "primary fish and working quay" ([Dart Harbour](https://www.dartharbour.org/commercial-ships/)), but no source places the 1967 fish landing, any crane, any coal store or the river-boat landing. A gasworks stood on the plot behind King's Quay, north of the centre, and its closure date was not found ([South Hams Today](https://www.southhams-today.co.uk/news/objections-to-dartmouth-development-plans-588273)). Mayor's Avenue keeps a pre-1885 warehouse "complete with hooks for unloading cargoes" ([South West Coast Path](https://www.southwestcoastpath.org.uk/walksdb/452/printable/)).

### Four names on today's map are wrong for 1967

*Confidence: medium. Each rests on one or two secondary sources; the cinema's name comes from a 1968 trade yearbook cited at second hand.*

The cinema on Mayor's Avenue at Zion Place traded as **"The Cinema"**. The White brothers took the lease in the mid-1960s and dropped the name Maxime; it became the Royalty only in April 1973, closed in August 1986 and was demolished for today's Dartmouth Clinic ([Cinema Treasures](https://cinematreasures.org/theaters/29900)). **The Cherub was not a pub.** The building was refurbished in 1958 by Cresswell Mullett and reopened as a private members' club, and it became a pub and restaurant only in the 1970s ([Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/photos/lemmo2009/49096250118); [Pubs Galore](https://www.pubsgalore.co.uk/pubs/7822/)). The harbour body was the **Dartmouth Harbour Commissioners**, not Dart Harbour; where its office stood in 1967 is unknown, and today's office at 6 Oxford Street is no evidence ([Dart Harbour](https://www.dartharbour.org/contact-us/opening-times/)). The town was the **Municipal Borough of Dartmouth**, which lasted from 1835 to 1974 ([Vision of Britain](https://test.visionofbritain.org.uk/unit/10042355)).

Other uses were as today or close to it. The museum had been upstairs in the Butterwalk since 1954 ([Dartmouth Museum](https://www.dartmouthmuseum.org/about/)). The purpose-built post office of 1908 at 9 South Embankment is now "the Old Post Office", and its closing date was not found, so it was probably still the post office ([British Post Office Buildings](http://britishpostofficearchitects.weebly.com/1908---south-embankment-9.html); [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1197576)). The Seven Stars and the Royal Castle were long-established houses ([CAMRA](https://camra.org.uk/pubs/seven-stars-dartmouth-148618); [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1218065)). No 1967 addresses were found for banks, chip shops or most shops. The Dartmouth History Research Group's book *Dartmouth Then – Dartmouth Now* compares the mid-1960s shops of Fairfax Place, the South Embankment, Duke Street and Foss Street with today's, shop by shop, and is the single best source for signwriting ([DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-books/dartmouth-then-dartmouth-now/)). Oral history recalls Hannaford's in Duke Street and Dunnings Bakery in Foss Street, without years ([DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/category/social-history/)).

### Listings give materials and outlines, not colours

*Confidence: high for fabric and dates; low for paint colours and street surfaces, for which no source was found.*

The landmark buildings looked in 1967 much as they do now. The **Royal Castle Hotel** has a stucco front with broad sashed bays and mock crenellations hiding two gabled houses of 1639, altered in 1840 ([Heritage Gateway](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/heritage-gateway/results/record/c39bc47e-3b72-4a06-9863-681629e456a2/MDV8546)). The **Butterwalk** of 1635–40 was "seriously damaged by blast in 1943, repaired and reinstated 1954" ([Heritage Gateway](https://www.heritagegateway.org.uk/gateway/Results_Single.aspx?uid=MDV24855&resourceID=104)). **St Saviour's** tower was heightened in 1633–37, but none of the summaries describes its top, so the "battlemented west tower" in `SOURCES.md` stays unverified ([Heritage Gateway](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/heritage-gateway/results/record/c39bc47e-3b72-4a06-9863-681629e456a2/MDV8551)). The **Old Custom House** at 4 Bayards Cove is a brick building similar to the Mansion House of 1736, so it should read as brick among rendered and slate-hung neighbours ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1197571)). The **Pannier Market** of 1828 was renovated around 1975, so 1967 shows its earlier state, with a cobbled yard around the Market Court House ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1297094); [Devon HCMTS](https://dartmouthplan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/J3-Devon-Historic-Coastal-and-Market-Towns-Survey.pdf)). **Britannia Royal Naval College**, built 1899–1905 by Sir Aston Webb in brick with Portland stone dressings, slate roofs and a stone clock cupola, had not changed ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1208626)).

Two cautions apply to artists working from present-day photographs. A fire in May 2010 damaged Fairfax Place and Higher Street, and the frontages there are reconstructions built to match, so they should be checked against pre-2010 pictures ([RIBA](https://find-an-architect.architecture.com/bbh-chartered-architects-limited/dartmouth/restoration-of-fairfax-place)). And Bayard's Cove in 1967 was a quiet quay of listed houses beside the fort of about 1510, before the BBC dressed it as Liverpool docks for *The Onedin Line* between 1971 and 1980 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayard%27s_Cove_Fort); [Explore Dartmouth](https://exploredartmouth.co.uk/dartmouth-film-tv-locations/)). Slate-hanging is a notable feature across the conservation area ([Conservation Area Appraisal](https://dartmouthplan.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Dartmouth_Appraisal_webfinal-2.pdf)). No source gives a facade palette or says which streets were setts and which tarmac.

### A borough of 5,758, with no regatta bunting in June

*Confidence: high for the census figure and the regatta's month; medium for the character sketch.*

The 1961 census counted 2,730 males and 3,028 females in Dartmouth parish, **5,758 people** ([Vision of Britain](https://test.visionofbritain.org.uk/unit/10072347/cube/GENDER)). The History Research Group describes the mid-1960s town as "small and self-contained, based on fishing and maritime trade, with a few tourist boats and the Britannia Royal Naval College" ([DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-books/dartmouth-then-dartmouth-now/)). The Royal Regatta is an August event held Thursday to Saturday around the last Friday of the month, which in 1967 was 25 August, so June has no bunting, fair or guardship ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Dartmouth_Royal_Regatta)). The nearest dated pictures the researchers could name, without seeing them, are a 1928 aerial view of the South Embankment and Boat Float ([Britain from Above](https://britainfromabove.org.uk/en/image/EPW024216)), the 1954 film *From Paignton to Dartmouth* ([BFI Player](https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/film/watch-from-paignton-to-dartmouth-1954-online)) and the Francis Frith collection ([Francis Frith](https://www.francisfrith.com/us/dartmouth/photos)).

## Kingswear was a British Railways terminus with a dead goods yard

### The station was staffed and signalled; freight had stopped two years earlier

*Confidence: high for dates and the listed fabric; low for the 1967 track plan, which nobody could open.*

Kingswear station opened on 16 August 1864. The Grade II listing describes a rectangular station building, a timber train shed with boarded sides, timber trusses with iron-rod bracing and a glazed ridge, clad in corrugated asbestos, and one long canopied platform ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1308783)). Wikipedia calls the shed a "rare wooden 100 feet (30 m) train shed" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)). **The signal box opened in 1894, received a new lever frame in 1960 and closed in October 1968**, so in June 1967 it was manned, and the researchers infer working semaphore signals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)). The railway ferry made a five-minute crossing from the pontoon beside the station ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)).

Freight had gone. **The wharf closed to freight on 4 May 1964 and ordinary goods traffic was withdrawn on 14 June 1965**; the old yard between the station and the waterfront is now a car park and boat storage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)). A local historian says the railway once comprised "all the land that is now the Marina" ([Reg Little](https://reglittle.wordpress.com/2011/11/27/the-railway-at-kingswear-in-the-1930s-and-1940s/)). Darthaven Marina Limited was incorporated only on 11 September 1975 ([CorpSignals](https://corpsignals.uk/companies/uk/darthaven-marina-ltd-01226089.html)). The 1967 waterfront north of the station is therefore idle railway ground: rusting sidings beside the river and no pontoons. That the track was still down in June 1967 is an inference, since a forum post says only that the quayside tracks were removed "later" ([RMweb](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/179823-kingswear-track-layout/)). Further north, Hoodown had a wartime jetty and fuel tanks for motor torpedo boats on a base the Free French used, and the harbour authority's depot there dates from the mid-1980s ([Kingswear Historians](https://kingswearhistorians.com/kingswear-history/kingswear-in-the-20th-century/hoodown-and-the-kingswear-golf-club/); [Dart Harbour](https://dartharbour.org/harbour-information/sailing-directions)).

### Diesel units had replaced steam on a Paignton shuttle

*Confidence: medium-high for the service pattern and the unit type; low for liveries and for through trains in 1967.*

From **18 April 1966 most trains were a shuttle from Paignton**, with through trains only at peak season, and regular Sunday trains ran for the last time in summer 1967, being withdrawn from 24 September ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)). The best-dated evidence for the train itself is a **Derby-built Class 116 three-car diesel multiple unit**, recorded on the 15.58 from Paignton in October 1966 and photographed on the branch around April 1967 ([railcar.co.uk](https://www.railcar.co.uk/operations/western-region/paignton-kingswear)). Steam had finished by about 1963–64 on a forum recollection only, and no documented last-steam date was found ([Railforums](https://www.railforums.co.uk/goto/post?id=4504910)). A Warship diesel-hydraulic on a summer-Saturday through train is plausible but unconfirmed for 1967; Warships then ran in green, maroon or the new blue ([Western Locomotive Association](https://westernlocomotives.com/shop/liveries-of-the-wr-diesel-hydraulics/)). No source gives the unit's 1967 livery or the station's sign colours.

This matters because the game's vendored renderer draws the opposite. Its own header describes "a GWR-style tank engine and seven chocolate-and-cream coaches, with smoke" on lines named "Dartmouth Steam Railway" (`src/render/railway.ts`). That is the heritage railway, which began running on 1 January 1973 after the line was sold on 30 December 1972 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway); [Kingsbridge Today](https://kingsbridge-today.co.uk/news/dart-valley-rail-celebrates-50-years-of-operation-599118)). Greenway Halt, which is in the game's railway data, opened in the 1990s or in 2012 depending on the source, and either way did not exist ([Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenway_Halt_railway_station_(Devon))). A one-coach halt of 1877 stood at the Higher Ferry level crossing, later called Britannia Halt; whether it was open in 1967 was not found ([Wikishire](https://wikishire.co.uk/wiki/Paignton_and_Dartmouth_Steam_Railway)).

### The village: grey wharf buildings, a hotel of unknown use and a private castle

*Confidence: high for the listed buildings; low for uses and colours in 1967.*

The **Royal Dart Hotel**, an Italianate stuccoed building of about 1850, stood between the station and the ferry slipway; whether it traded as a hotel or bar in 1967 was not found, and it has since become apartments ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1146644); [Hydrock](https://www.hydrock.com/projects/royal-dart-dartmouth)). The **Royal Dart Yacht Club** occupied its purpose-built clubhouse of 1881, which was refurbished in 1974 and redeveloped in 1991, so the 1967 building predates both changes; whether the club had a pontoon then is unknown ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dart_Yacht_Club)). The church of St Thomas of Canterbury has a medieval slate-rubble tower and a body rebuilt in 1847 in local limestone rubble ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1108544)). **Kingswear Castle was a private house**, restored as a summer residence in 1855 and bought by the Landmark Trust only in 1987, so it should look lived in ([Landmark Trust](https://cms.landmarktrust.org.uk/globalassets/3.-images-and-documents-to-keep/history-albums/kingswear-castle-history-album.pdf)).

Colour is the trap at Kingswear. One source says the riverside wharf buildings were "drab looking" grey limestone and have since been rendered, painted in bright colours and converted to apartments ([Bambrick Studio](https://bambrickstudio.co.uk/kingswear/)). The researchers found no source saying today's pastel house colours existed in 1967. The nearest reference views are black and white: Frith's "Kingswear, c1960" and "Dartmouth, from Kingswear 1957" ([Francis Frith](https://www.francisfrith.com/kingswear/kingswear-c1960_k34001/buy); [Francis Frith](https://www.francisfrith.com/dartmouth/dartmouth-from-kingswear-1957_d7094/buy)).

### The harbour mouth had a taller stone lighthouse and an empty battery

*Confidence: high that the old lighthouse existed and has gone; low for its shape and exact position; medium for the battery.*

The clearest lost feature at the mouth is the **Kingswear lighthouse of 1864: a white-painted stone tower about 11 m (36 ft) high, demolished as unsafe in 1980** and replaced in 1981 by today's small white glass-fibre tower ([Lighthouse Digest](https://www.lighthousedepot.com/database/uniquelighthouse.cfm?value=1917); [Lighthouses of SW England](https://ibiblio.org/lighthouse/engsw.htm); [mycetes.co.uk](https://www.mycetes.co.uk/a/page65.html)). Sources call it hexagonal or octagonal. Its position needs care. One guide describes it "as part of Kingswear Castle", but the position the game's code gives the present light (`src/sim/env/shoreLights.ts`) is about (371, −131), some 800 m north-west of the Kingswear Castle landmark at (963, −666). Since the 1981 tower replaced the 1864 one at the same light station, the old tower most probably stood at the present light's site, not at the castle. That is this report's inference from the game's data, not a research finding, and a 1960s map must settle it before anyone models the tower.

The rest of the mouth is unchanged in outline. The **Daymark** is an open-based octagonal tower about 24 m (80 ft) high, built in 1864 on the hill above Froward Point and unlit ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1108546); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_Daymark)). Dartmouth Castle and St Petrox church stand on the west bank ([English Heritage](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/dartmouth-castle/history/)). East of the mouth, **Brownstone Battery** was a Second World War coastal battery decommissioned in 1956, so in 1967 it was eleven years empty; its observation post became a National Coastwatch lookout only later ([National Trust](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/devon/dartmouth-and-kingswear-countryside/the-history-of-brownstone-battery)). The game data labels that building "Coastwatch Lookout Station" at (2160, −1257); the structure should stay and the modern use should go.

The River Dart Steamboat Co. had been owned since 1952 by the Welsh firm Evans & Reid, which is why its two newest boats carry Welsh castle names ([Simplon](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html)). Its steamers moored overnight on the Kingswear side, head up-river, and each of the three paddlers carried about 500 passengers, according to an account of the early 1960s ([kingswearcastle.org](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/potm/october-2004-kingswear-castle-on-the-river-dart-in-the-early-1960s/)). The game draws its Totnes boat as *Cardiff Castle*, which is the right ship, but in the dark blue of today's operator (`src/render/traffic.ts`). Her 1967 colours were not found. One mirror of the Wikipedia article says the company's funnels were yellow with a black top after 1906, which is the only lead and a weak one ([Wikipedia mirror](https://xxg.pages.dev/0xL1JpdmVyX0RhcnRfU3RlYW1ib2F0X0NvX0x0ZA)). A second operator, G. H. Riddalls, ran "Red Cruisers" with red hulls and white superstructures at an unstated period ([Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/Dart_Pleasure_Craft.html)). No 1967 timetable or landing stage was confirmed. For the game, the important picture is the two paddle steamers: *Kingswear Castle* silent in Old Mill Creek, just sold and waiting for her tow, and the rusting hulk of *Totnes Castle* on a river mooring whose position is unknown.

### The shipyard was at Noss, and Sandquay was already a marina

*Confidence: medium. The researchers' notes disagree with each other here, and this section takes the dated sources.*

Philip & Son had built ships at Sandquay since 1858 and took over the Noss works in 1918 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_and_Son)). In 1958 the firm decided to **move all shipyard work to Noss and develop Sandquay as a marina**; the yard's former offices and workshops became a **19-bedroom hotel in June 1961**, and "the Sandquay site becomes a marina" in 1965 ([Dart Marina](https://dartmarina.com/history/); [DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/a-brief-history-of-dartmouth/)). The hotel was enlarged to 50 bedrooms in 1994 and the Dart Marina Apartments followed in the early 2000s ([Explore Dartmouth](https://exploredartmouth.co.uk/dartmouth-stories-dart-marina-hotel/)). One research note suggested modelling building slips and a hull on the stocks at Sandquay; on the dated evidence that picture belongs at Noss. In 1967 Sandquay should show a modest marina, a small hotel in old yard buildings, the Higher Ferry slip and the College's boat landing. The game is wrong in the opposite direction in its documents: `SOURCES.md` says "the marinas date from the 1970s onwards", which is false for Dart Marina.

**Noss in June 1967 was a working yard.** Its recorded output runs through the decade: Bar Light Vessel 23 (1960), Light Vessel 21 and *Conway Castle* (1963), the Lower Ferry tugs (1965 and 1966) and *Shannon Heather* (1968) ([Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Ships_built_at_Philip_%26_Son,_Dartmouth); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Lower_Ferry)). As of 1961 the yard could build vessels up to about 280 ft ([Grace's Guide](https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/Philip_and_Son)). Dartmouth Museum's statement that "the shipbuilding side closed in 1964" conflicts with those builds and with an end date of October 1999 ([Dartmouth Museum](https://www.dartmouthmuseum.org/product/philip-son/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_and_Son)). The present marina at Noss opened officially on 19 May 2023 ([Marine Industry News](https://marineindustrynews.co.uk/premier-marinas-noss-on-dart-officially-opens/)). No source describes the yard's 1960s sheds, cranes or slip count; the only picture named is a 1928 aerial view ([Britain from Above](https://britainfromabove.org.uk/en/image/EPW024184)). The coal-bunkering trade should not appear. The harbour's coaling tug *Portwey* was sold away in 1951, and no source shows working coal hulks in the 1960s ([engineers.scot](https://engineers.scot/news/2021-02-09-portwey-the-history-of-a-west-country-steam-tug)).

### Type 12 frigates, and no blue-and-white picket boats

*Confidence: high for the squadron's ships; low for whether any lay in the river in early June; medium that the modern college boats are wrong.*

The Dartmouth Training Squadron was the 17th Frigate Squadron. In 1967 it comprised the Type 12 (Whitby-class) frigates **HMS *Torquay*, *Scarborough*, *Eastbourne* and *Tenby***, led from *Torquay* by Captain Ronald Forrest between August 1966 and December 1967 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Frigate_Squadron_(United_Kingdom)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Scarborough_(F63))). Its cruises fell in college terms, so the ships may have been away for part of June, and no source confirms a frigate on the river in 1967. Dart Harbour today describes "large mainstream Admiralty mooring buoys" in mid-river, which is where a grey frigate would credibly lie ([Dart Harbour](https://www.dartharbour.org/harbour-information/anchoring/)).

The college's boats are a period trap. Trade press says the "distinctive blue and white picket boats had been operating on the River Dart since the 1960s" ([Marine Industry News](https://marineindustrynews.co.uk/royal-navy-replaces-historic-training-boats/)). The National Historic Ships register, however, records picket boat PB7 as **built in 1975**, number 7 of 8 ([National Historic Ships](https://www.nationalhistoricships.org.uk/register/3866/pb7)). The register is the better record, so those boats must not appear. Earlier picket boats did exist, since *Navy News* reported them racing during a royal visit in 1962 ([Navy News](https://archive.org/stream/NavyNews1962Collection/196208_djvu.txt)). The present whalers were built between 1970 and 1989 ([Royal Navy](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/news/2017/june/29/170629-new-lease-of-life-for-brnc-boats)). The moored training ship *Hindostan* is a former Sandown-class minehunter, a 21st-century arrangement ([BRNC article mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/BRNC_Dartmouth.html)). The game draws her off Sandquay at (−366, 1333) with no era gate (`src/render/dart.ts`), so she must come out. What replaced these in 1967 is unsourced: an informal forum post lists rowing skiffs, sailing dinghies, cutters, whalers and a motor cutter at Sandquay, without a date ([PPRuNe](https://www.pprune.org/8196611-post4704.html)).

### Fishing boats, yachts and moorings are the thinnest evidence

*Confidence: low throughout. Nothing specific to 1967 was found.*

The searches found nothing on the 1960s Dartmouth fishing fleet, the pilot boat, the Commissioners' launches or commercial tugs; many results confused the town with its namesakes in Massachusetts and Nova Scotia. The researchers' picture of a handful of wooden inshore crabbers is general knowledge and should be labelled as an assumption in the game's notes. For yachts, the Royal Dart Yacht Club was promoting one-design dinghy racing after the war and launched its Junior Sailing Week in 1959 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dart_Yacht_Club)). No 1967 mooring count exists in the notes. Even today's figure is disputed, at about 2,800 moorings in one article and 1,600 plus 270 pontoon berths in another ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Harbour); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_Harbour_%26_Navigation_Authority)). The game keeps 42% of moored sailing boats and about 17% of motor boats, which leaves roughly 165 of the 443 boats in the data. That is a design judgement, not a sourced figure, and it should stay labelled as one.

### Two harbour bodies, and buoys that need a 1960s chart

*Confidence: high for the authorities; low for every buoy and light as it was in 1967.*

Two statutory bodies ran the river in 1967: the **Dartmouth Harbour Commissioners** under the Dartmouth Harbour Act 1951 and the **River Dart Navigation Commissioners** under the River Dart Navigation Act 1962. The Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority Act 1975 merged them ([vLex](https://legislation.vlex.co.uk/vid/808182321)). Harbour launches, notices and uniforms should therefore carry the Commissioners' name. Wikipedia describes two fixed leading lights 110 ft apart at the entrance, without a date ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Harbour)). Today's buoys are a green conical buoy at Castle Ledge and red cans at the Checkstone and Homestone ([Visit My Harbour](https://www.visitmyharbour.com/harbour/dartmouth-marinas/); [Royal Dart YC](https://royaldart.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Avoiding_Rocks.pdf)). The game places Castle Ledge as a green starboard-hand buoy flashing green from the same modern source, and gives the Kingswear and Bayard's Cove lights their modern sector characters (`public/geo/features.json`, `src/sim/env/shoreLights.ts`). None of that is evidence for 1967. The researchers flag, from memory and without a source, that British buoyage then predated today's colour system, so a starboard-hand buoy was not necessarily green. Admiralty chart 2253 in a 1960s edition is the document that settles buoys, lights and the old lighthouse in one sitting.

## Streets, people and sound belong to a transition year

### Vehicles: British-built, sparse, and no plate newer than an E

*Confidence: high for plates, market leaders and the two photographed buses; low for liveries and for anything specific to Dartmouth's streets.*

The Ford Cortina, in its Mk2 form launched in late 1966, was Britain's best-selling car in 1967, ending the run of the BMC 1100, which had led in 1965 and 1966 ([Best Selling Cars Blog](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1968/01/uk-1967-ford-cortina-takes-the-lead/); [Best Selling Cars Blog](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1967/01/uk-1965-1966-austin-morris-11001300-in-charge/)). Great Britain had **14.1 million licensed vehicles** of all types in the third quarter of 1967, which supports the game's choice to thin the parked cars ([Commercial Motor](https://archive.commercialmotor.com/article/10th-may-1968/65/what-are-the-most-recent-figures-available-as-to-t)). BMC offered Old English White and Almond Green from 1958 to 1967, which supports the muted palette already in `src/render/population.ts` ([Classic Car Hub](https://classiccarhub.co.uk/austin-classic-paint-colour-codes/)). **The newest suffix letter in early June 1967 was E**, used from 1 January to 31 July; F began on 1 August ([CarReg](https://www.carreg.co.uk/suffix-number-plates/)). Plates were black with white or silver characters, because reflective plates became optional only in 1968, although one source says 1967 ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/British_number_plates); [Tractors wiki](https://tractors.fandom.com/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_the_UK)). Devon office codes included DV, TT, TA, OD and UO, so a new local car might read "xDV 123E" ([Primoreg](https://www.primoreg.co.uk/history/registration-code/dv)).

Dartmouth's buses were **Western National**. Bristol K-type double-decker no. 928 was photographed on the South Embankment in September 1966 and was withdrawn in July 1967, and a Bristol K6B was photographed at Dartmouth Depot in 1968 ([SCT '61](https://www.sct61.org.uk/index/operator/wn)). A forum calls Western National a "green Tilling" company, which is the only livery evidence ([RMweb](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/60694-buses-in-cornwall-in-1960s-1970s/page/3/)). Torbay was Devon General territory, with Leyland Atlanteans and the open-top "Sea Dogs" of 1961, and whether its buses reached Kingswear was not found ([Commercial Motor](https://archive.commercialmotor.com/article/19th-may-1961/80/sea-dogs-for-sunshine-coast)). The Post Office's standard small van was the Morris Minor, about 39,417 having been supplied by October 1969 ([British Telephones](https://www.britishtelephones.com/vehicles/minorvans/023.htm)). AA patrols used Mini vans as standard from about 1965 ([Honest John](https://classics.honestjohn.co.uk/best-classics/classic-quirks/classic-aa-vehicles/)). Panda cars were spreading from a 1965 pilot, but none is evidenced in Dartmouth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panda_car)). The car and boat shapes in the game remain modern, as `SOURCES.md` already admits.

### Street furniture: two generations of road sign, and only K6 kiosks

*Confidence: high for the national rules; medium for Dartmouth's listed lamp posts and kiosks; low for lantern type, railings, benches and surfaces.*

1967 is a transition year for signs. The Worboys regulations came into force on 1 January 1965 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worboys_Committee)). In February 1967 a minister said "all regulatory signs must be changed by the end of this year", funds permitting, and in 1970 an MP was still asking why the report had not been fully implemented ([Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1967/feb/01/new-signs); [Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1970/may/28/worboys-committees-report)). Fingerposts were not affected, on a forum's word ([SABRE](https://www.sabre-roads.org.uk/forum/viewtopic.php?p=1193809)). A level designer can therefore mix new Worboys regulatory signs at main junctions with older warning signs and black-and-white fingerposts. **Every telephone kiosk is a red K6**, because the K8 was first installed in July 1968 ([BT Archives](https://www.bt.com/bt-plc/assets/documents/about-bt/our-history/bt-archives/information-sheets-and-timelines/the-development-of-the-k8-kiosk.pdf)). Dartmouth has listed K6s on Victoria Road, at Coles Court and in the castle grounds, though their installation dates were not found ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1297043); [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1297081); [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1197565)). Postboxes carry the cipher they were cast with, from VR to EIIR ([Letter Box Study Group](https://lbsg.org/about-boxes/ciphers)).

The Boat Float's two listed cast-iron lamp posts were standing, so ornate cast-iron columns around the Float and the Quay are correct ([Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1218145); [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1197588)). Whether they carried gas or electric lanterns in 1967 is unknown; the town had a public electricity supply from 1902 on a weak source ([WPEHS](https://wpehs.org.uk/early-public-electricity-supplies-in-the-south-west)). Nationally, concrete columns and orange low-pressure sodium were the 1960s norm on main roads ([SABRE](https://www.sabre-roads.org.uk/wiki/index.php?title=SOX)). For a garage forecourt, the sourced 1967 brands are Shell and BP ([Grace's Guide](https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/Shell-Mex_and_B._P.)), National, the rebranded National Benzole ([Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Benzole)), Regent, American-owned by 1967 ([Motor Sport](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/august-1967/11/petrol/)), and Fina ([Motor Sport](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/february-1953/22/enthusiasts-directory-no-5-branded-petrol/)). Esso's Cleveland stations kept their own name until the early 1970s on a photo caption's word, and one motorist first saw a Gulf station "around 1968" ([Alamy](https://www.alamy.es/1954-historical-exterior-view-of-an-ancient-elizabethan-building-essex-england-uk-with-a-large-forecourt-and-petrol-pumps-fuels-sold-included-clevecol-and-aero-with-a-sign-for-cleveland-petrols-cleveland-was-a-british-company-originally-established-in-1920-at-trafford-park-in-manchester-by-norman-davis-which-in-1938-was-majority-purchased-by-the-us-business-the-anglo-american-oil-co-in-1951-anglo-american-began-calling-itself-esso-after-its-orgins-as-standard-oil-eastern-seaboard-standard-oil-in-the-early-1970s-all-cleveland-filling-stations-were-rebranded-esso-image432891503.html); [Sunday in Old Money](https://sundayinoldmoney.blogspot.com/2017/05)). No source was found for enamel advertisements, shopfront lettering, bus-stop flags, newspaper placards or parking on the Embankment.

### People: conventional dress, a brand-new police force and white-topped caps

*Confidence: medium for the police and naval facts; low for civilian dress, which rests on one enthusiast site and mostly American hair sources.*

Provincial dress lagged London. One source says fashion "was still regional in the 1960s", and that for mainstream youth "everything changed again in the Summer of 1967" as the mod look gave way to longer hair, beads and kaftans ([Retrowow](https://www.retrowow.co.uk/retro_style/60s/60s_fashion_men.php)). A Dartmouth crowd in early June should therefore be mostly conventional, with jackets, ties, knee-length skirts and headscarves among the old and shorter skirts and longer fringes among the young; the specific garments are art direction, not findings. Women's hair was dominated by the backcombed bouffant on sources that are mainly American ([Hair and Makeup Artist](https://hair-and-makeup-artist.com/womens-1960s-hairstyles/)).

Uniforms are better anchored. **The Devon and Cornwall Constabulary was formed on 1 June 1967** according to the force's own history as reported in the press, though Wikipedia says 1 April ([Cornish Times](https://www.cornish-times.co.uk/news/999/police-mark-50th-anniversary-129458); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_and_Cornwall_Police)). On the force's date, the constable on the Quay on 3 June belongs to a force two days old, and what badge he wore is not known. Royal Navy ratings had worn white-topped caps all year round since 1956 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniforms_of_the_Royal_Navy)). Midshipmen wear a white collar patch ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy_officer_rank_insignia)). One history says cadets did not frequent the town much, although many of the college's staff lived there, without saying which era it describes ([Naval Marine Archive](https://navalmarinearchive.com/research/navies/britannia_college.html)). What Dartmouth cadets, postmen, fishermen and shop workers wore in 1967 was not found; the National Museum of the Royal Navy's uniform guide is the place to start ([NMRN](https://www.nmrn.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-08/Research%20Guide%20-%20Identifying%20Uniforms.pdf)).

### Sounds: the Light Programme, a ferry bell and no steam

*Confidence: high for radio and chart dates; medium for the vehicle and vessel sounds, which follow from the sourced machinery; nothing was found on church bells or gulls.*

In early June 1967 radio meant the BBC Light Programme, which was split into Radio 1 and Radio 2 only on 30 September ([Electronics Notes](https://www.electronics-notes.com/articles/history/broadcasting/pirate-radio-broadcasting-in-1960s-radio-caroline.php)). The offshore pirates were still on air, since the Marine Broadcasting Offences Act took effect at midnight on 14 August ([National Archives](https://blog.nationalarchives.gov.uk/august-14-1967-midnight-showdown-pop-pirates/)). Whether they could be heard in Devon was not checked. **Procol Harum's "A Whiter Shade of Pale" entered the top ten in the week ending 7 June and was number one from the week ending 14 June**; Engelbert Humperdinck's "There Goes My Everything" and the Supremes' "The Happening" were also in the June top ten ([Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/List_of_UK_top-ten_singles_in_1967)). *Sgt. Pepper* was days old, released on 26 May or 1 June depending on the source ([Beatles Bible](https://beatlesbible.com/1967/06/01/uk-release-sgt-peppers-lonely-hearts-club-band); [Lakes Media Network](https://www.lakesmedianetwork.com/classic-rock/on-this-day-may-26-1967-the-beatles-release-sgt-peppers-lonely-hearts-club-band/)). Ice-cream vans played chimes from music boxes that Tonibell introduced, with Greensleeves the archetypal tune ([Lancaster Insurance](https://www.lancasterinsurance.co.uk/news/2021/august/12/12-facts-about-the-british-ice-cream-van/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensleeves)).

The sourced machinery gives the sound designer a distinct river. The Higher Ferry was a diesel-electric paddle vessel, so its sound is a generator and beating paddles, not the present ferry's. The trains were diesel units, so there is no chuff and no steam whistle at Kingswear, although a manned signal box was still at work. The buses included ageing Bristol K-types. The Dittisham ferry was called by a shore bell. Both paddle steamers were silent, and Noss was a working shipyard. No source was found for St Saviour's bell-ringing, for gull species or numbers, or for the legal hours of ice-cream chimes in 1967.

A verification pass should run in that order of leverage. One late-1960s Ordnance Survey sheet settles the seven undated North Embankment blocks, the gasworks, the cinema, the sidings and the lighthouse position at once. One Admiralty chart settles the buoys and lights. The History Research Group's two books settle the shops and the crossings. The Simplon pages and the 1964 film settle most vessel colours. Allowing the blocked hosts in the session's network settings, starting with en.wikipedia.org, historicengland.org.uk, maps.nls.uk, simplonpc.co.uk, kingswearcastle.org and dartmouth-history.org.uk, would let the cited pages be read in full and every "high" in this report be confirmed or struck.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the period work should go. The game's 1967 effort so far has been subtraction: take modern buildings and pontoons off a present-day map. The evidence says the buildings were the easy part and are largely done, while almost every moving object in the scene is still present-day: a 2009 cable ferry, a modern passenger launch, a heritage steam train, a 21st-century training ship, and buoys and lights taken from a modern pilot guide. A player will read period from the ferry crossing in front of them long before they notice a supermarket at Townstal. Replacing the Higher Ferry, the passenger ferry, the train and the station pontoon, and adding the two dead paddle steamers, would buy more 1967 than any further building removals.

The chosen week is also richer than a generic "1967". Early June catches a set of things that were about to vanish: *Kingswear Castle* sold and waiting for her tow, the hulk of *Totnes Castle* five months from sinking, a Bristol K-type bus a month from withdrawal, the last summer of Sunday trains, a signal box sixteen months from closure, a police force perhaps days old and a radio dial that would be rearranged by autumn. The town was at the end of one era and had not yet become the yachting and television backdrop it is now. That gives the writers and the sound designer real material, provided the verification pass comes first: every one of those details is, for now, a search summary of a page nobody on the project has read.
