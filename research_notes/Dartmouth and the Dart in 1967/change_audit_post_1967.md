# Change audit: Dartmouth, Kingswear and the lower Dart, June 1967 versus today

Method note (applies throughout): researched 19 November 2026. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (en.wikipedia.org, darthaven.co.uk, dartmouthplan.org and others were refused by the network proxy), so every finding below rests on search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not on a full reading. The National Library of Scotland OS maps, Britain from Above and Historic England aerial archives could not be opened. "Verified" here means stated in at least one cited source's extract. Positions in game terms are SailSim local metres (east, north) of the Royal Dart Yacht Club, taken from `public/geo/features.json`.

## 1. Dartmouth buildings and developments after 1967 (North Embankment blocks, Flavel, Newcomen/Visitor Centre, lifeboat station, Townstal, school, M&S)

### Takeaway
Most entries in the game's Dartmouth removal list are correctly post-1967 where a date could be found (Kings Quay House 1975, the lifeboat station 2007, the Flavel 2005, the health centre 2023, the Academy building 2010). But the Newcomen engine house (1963–64) was standing in 1967 and must stay. The Flavel's site held a rebuilt Methodist church hall in 1967. The North Embankment flats area held a gasworks site, and before 1930 boatbuilding yards.

### Inferences
- **The North Embankment flats.** The blocks along Clarence Street and the North Embankment (Kings Quay House, Dartside Court, Mayflower Court, Lee Court, Vavasour House, Sails, Clarence House) stand around the gasworks plot. The one dated block is from 1975, so the 1967 scene here was probably the gasworks, small industrial and commercial buildings, and older houses on Clarence Street rather than open ground. This is unverified. The gasworks' closure date and the 1967 footprints need the 1:2500 OS sheets (NLS, c.1950s–1970s editions).
- **The Flavel site.** Removing The Flavel leaves a hole that in 1967 held the rebuilt Methodist church hall. A smaller two-storey hall-sized block on the same plot is the right fill. Its footprint is unknown.
- **The new lifeboat station.** It is in a building that already existed in 1967: the 1870s lifeboat house at Sandquay, then the rowing club. If a later OSM pull names it "Dart Lifeboat Station", it must be kept, not removed by name.
- **Dartmouth House and M&S.** "Dartmouth House" (Mayor's Avenue, company c.1998) and the M&S "Mayors Court" block are candidates for post-1967 buildings at the Mayor's Avenue / The Quay corner. The game currently keeps both. They need checking against a 1960s OS sheet.
- **Townstal.** The Townstal retail and leisure cluster (Sainsbury's, Lidl, BP/Spar, the leisure centre, the park-and-ride, the health centre) has no exact dates except the health centre (2023). Large modern retail of this kind is very unlikely to predate 1967. Removing it is a reasonable call, but it is not individually verified.

### Gaps

- No opening dates found for Sainsbury's, the BP/Spar filling station, the park-and-ride, the leisure centre, M&S, or the Visitor Centre extension.
- When Dartmouth's gasworks closed, and whether a gasholder still stood in June 1967, was not found.
- Townstal housing estates are unnamed in OSM and undated. Which estates postdate 1967 needs OS map comparison.

## 2. Kingswear: Darthaven Marina, Marina View, RDYC pontoons, Hoodown, new housing

### Takeaway
Darthaven Marina is post-1967 (founded in the 1970s; the exact year was not found). Its site was railway land: the Kingswear goods yard and quay, plus a fish quay. In June 1967 that yard had just lost its traffic (wharf freight ended 1964, goods withdrawn 14 June 1965) but was still railway property. Marina View Apartments probably date from c.1990. All the floating pontoons on the Kingswear side are almost certainly later than 1967, but none was dated.

### Cited Findings
- **Darthaven's age.** The marina's own site says "for over 50 years" ([Darthaven](https://darthaven.co.uk/marina)). Its 2024 yearbook says it has been established "for more than 45 years" and is approaching its 50th anniversary ([Darthaven Yearbook 2024](https://www.darthaven.co.uk/userfiles/downloads/DarthavenYearbook2024MASTER.pdf)). These put the founding at roughly the mid-to-late 1970s. Darthaven Marina Limited is Companies House company 01226089 ([Companies House](https://pay.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/01226089/officers)).
- **Kingswear goods yard.**
  - Freight was rail-served from 1866. New sidings were laid in 1894, with more in 1895 on reclaimed land north of the station.
  - The wharf stopped taking freight in 1964, and ordinary goods traffic was withdrawn on 14 June 1965.
  - The old goods yard between the station and the waterfront is now a car park and boat storage. A few sidings are retained opposite the platform and towards Britannia Crossing.
  - Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station).
- **The marina was railway land.**
  - A wiki mirror of the Dartmouth Steam Railway article says the site is now part of Darthaven Marina and "was once a busy rail-served quayside goods yard" ([Justapedia mirror](https://justapedia.org/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway)). This is a mirror, so less authoritative.
  - A local historian says the railway comprised "all the land that is now the Marina" in the 1930s–40s ([Reg Little](https://reglittle.wordpress.com/2011/11/27/the-railway-at-kingswear-in-the-1930s-and-1940s/)).
  - An AONB walk leaflet says that "until fairly recently a fish quay occupied the area where the boatyard and its associated marina are now" ([South Devon AONB, Kingswear and Waterhead Creek](https://www.southdevon-nl.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/OS-kingswear-and-waterhead-creek.pdf)).
- **Ownership of the line.** The Paignton–Kingswear line stayed British Railways until it was sold to the Dart Valley Railway on 30 December 1972 ([Wikipedia, Dartmouth Steam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway); [Explore Devon](https://www.exploredevon.info/activities/walk/short-walks/the-dart-circuit/)).
- **Marina View, Kingswear (206, 157).**
  - Leasehold flats on 999-year leases. One listing gives the lease start as 1990, another (No. 3) 1995.
  - The flats sit above a commercial unit in the conservation area, with views of the marina and river.
  - Sources: [Waterside Properties](https://www.watersideproperties.com/property/marina-view-kingswear-devon/); [The Coastal House](https://thecoastalhouse.co.uk/property/3-marina-view-kingswear/).
- **Riverside wharf buildings.** Kingswear's "drab looking Riverside wharf buildings" were built of local grey limestone. They have since been rendered, painted in bright colours and converted into apartments ([Bambrick Studio](https://bambrickstudio.co.uk/kingswear/), attribution from search extract; date not given).
- **Hoodown.** A wartime jetty was built at Hoodown to refuel MTBs and MLs, supplied from three large tanks below Hoodown Lane ([Reg Little](https://reglittle.wordpress.com/tag/kingswear-history-remembered/); [Kingswear Historians](https://kingswearhistorians.com/kingswear-history/kingswear-in-the-20th-century/hoodown-and-the-kingswear-golf-club/)). No date was found for the Dart Harbour (DHNA) Hoodown depot building at (212, 441) or its pontoon.

### Inferences
- **Darthaven company number (inference, unverified).** Company 01226089 falls in the run of numbers issued around 1975, which fits the "approaching 50 years" claim. Treat c.1975–77 as the probable founding. Everything in Darthaven's basin is post-1967: its pontoons, boatyard sheds, offices, chandlery and the Marina View block.
- **The Darthaven site in June 1967 (to add back, approximate).** Railway goods-yard ground with sidings, between the station platform (about (21–212, 66–240)) and the river, plus the fish quay on the waterfront. Traffic had ended two years earlier, so the tracks may still have been in place but unused. Whether they had been lifted by June 1967 is unverified.
- **Marina View Apartments (206, 157).** Probably built c.1990, which agrees with the removal list. The lease start dates may not equal the build year.
- **Kingswear's riverside stone warehouses.** These stood in 1967 as bare grey limestone wharf buildings, so in 1967 colours they should be keeps, painted grey stone.

### Gaps
- Exact Darthaven opening year, and what its first pontoons and buildings were.
- When the goods-yard tracks were lifted, and whether the fish quay was active in 1967.
- Dates for the RDYC pontoons ("Royal Dart Yacht Club pontoon" and the "Moody Island" dinghy pontoon), the Hoodown depot, and the KP–KU Kingswear pontoons.
- Post-1967 housing in Kingswear: no source found. OSM does not date it.

## 3. The river: marinas, visitor and Embankment pontoons, ferry slipways, town jetty, moorings

### Takeaway
The biggest correction: Dart Marina at Sandquay already existed in June 1967. The shipyard moved to Noss from 1958, the hotel opened in June 1961, and the marina dates from 1965. It was smaller than today's, and the hotel was enlarged in 1994, with apartments added in the early 2000s. Noss was still Philip & Son's working shipyard in 1967; the leisure marina came after shipbuilding ended in October 1999, and the present one opened in May 2023. The station pontoon was a covered wooden pontoon until 1986. Several modern pontoons, including the rebuilt Double Steps pontoon, are post-1967.

### Cited Findings
- **Dart Marina at Sandquay.**
  - In 1958 it was decided to move all shipyard work to Noss and develop Sandquay as a marina, run by a new company, Dart Marina Ltd ([Dart Marina history](https://dartmarina.com/history/)).
  - The shipyard's former offices and workshops were converted into a 19-bedroom hotel, opened in June 1961 ([Dart Marina history](https://dartmarina.com/history/)). A hotel directory also gives "Year Built: 1961" ([Successful Meetings](https://www.successfulmeetings.com/Meeting-Event-Venues/Dartmouth-England/Convention-Hotel/The-Dart-Marina-p60603871)).
  - "The Sandquay site becomes a marina": 1965 ([Dartmouth History Research Group timeline](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/a-brief-history-of-dartmouth/)).
  - Trust House appointed managers in 1967 ([Dart Marina history](https://dartmarina.com/history/)).
  - In 1994 Richard Seton spent £1.5m refurbishing and enlarging the hotel to 50 bedrooms. In the early 2000s the Dart Marina Apartments were built and the marina updated ([Explore Dartmouth](https://exploredartmouth.co.uk/dartmouth-stories-dart-marina-hotel/); [Dart Marina history](https://dartmarina.com/history/)).
  - Premier Marinas has since bought the Dart Marina Group (marina, hotel and Higher Ferry) ([Premier Marinas](https://www.premiermarinas.com/news/premier-marinas-acquires-dart-marina-group)).
  - Conflict: one account dates the hotel company to 1970 and Trust House involvement to then ([Dart Marina history](https://dartmarina.com/history/), as summarised).
- **Philip & Son.**
  - Shipbuilders at Sandquay from 1858. They took over Noss Works from Simpson, Strickland in 1918.
  - Ship and boat building ended in October 1999.
  - Sources: [Wikipedia, Philip and Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_and_Son); [Grace's Guide](https://gracesguide.co.uk/Philip_and_Son).
- **Noss on Dart Marina.**
  - Premier Marinas bought the 37-acre former Philip & Son site out of administration in 2016 ([PBO](https://www.pbo.co.uk/news/premier-marinas-acquires-noss-marina-river-dart-28674)).
  - Phase one opened officially on 19 May 2023: a floating marina, a full-service boatyard with dry-stack storage, two commercial tenant buildings, and a South Devon College Marine Academy base. Phase two will redevelop the waterside, "previously home to the Philips building" ([Marine Industry News](https://marineindustrynews.co.uk/premier-marinas-noss-on-dart-officially-opens/); [Premier Marinas](https://www.premiermarinas.com/news/official-opening-at-noss-on-dart)).
  - In between, Noss Marina Ltd ran it as a leisure marina; it sponsored a 2009 documentary on the yard ([Wikipedia, Philip and Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_and_Son)).
- **Dartmouth station pontoon.** In 1986, when the Embankment was rebuilt and extended outward for flood prevention, the covered wooden pontoon was removed. It was replaced by a larger open pontoon of concrete and steel, which the passenger ferry and the cruise boats still use ([Wikipedia, Dartmouth railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_railway_station); [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)).
- **Embankment widening.** The Embankment was widened by 6 m and raised between 1985 and 1990 to stop flooding at spring tides. Source: search extract drawing on [Wikipedia, Dartmouth, Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon) and the [Dartmouth Conservation Area Appraisal](https://dartmouthplan.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Dartmouth_Appraisal_webfinal-2.pdf). Which of the two states it was not confirmed.
- **Higher Ferry.** Early in 2008 the floating bridge company contracted a replacement, a conventional cable ferry ([Wikipedia, Dartmouth Higher Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry)). Both slipways were extended, using sheet-piled cofferdams ([TMS Maritime](https://www.tmsmaritime.co.uk/services/marine-construction/slipway-construction-repairs/), undated).
- **Deep Water Pontoons DW1–DW3.** They lie on the Kingswear side, above the fuel barge and south of the Higher Ferry ([Dart Harbour](https://www.dartharbour.org/annual-berth-holders/mooring-locations/dartmouth-and-kingswear/); [eOceanic](https://eoceanic.com/sailing/harbours/534/)). No installation date was found.
- **Double Steps pontoon.** A new pontoon at Double Steps "provides new deep water berths for the yacht taxi and ferries" ([Kingsbridge Today](https://www.kingsbridge-today.co.uk/news/new-pontoon-626136); undated in extract, apparently c.2023).
- **Cruise-ship tender landing.** A further new floating tender landing in central Dartmouth was first used by Silver Wind passengers in 2026 ([Seatrade Cruise](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/ports-destinations/dart-harbour-opens-new-tender-landing-for-visitors)).

### Inferences
- **Dart Marina.** The game's docstring says Dart Marina's berths are removed, but in the data its pontoons are tagged non-floating. `features1967()` therefore keeps them and only drops the label (`marinas: []`). That accident suits 1967 better than full removal, since a marina existed from 1965. The present layout is probably larger than the 1965 one, but no 1960s plan was found.
- **The hotel at Sandquay.** The present hotel footprint includes the 1994 extension, and the adjacent Dart Marina Apartments are early 2000s. If the hotel and apartments are separate unnamed buildings in the data, the apartments are candidates for removal.

Some old sheds may survive in the data (unnamed large buildings near (-188, 2106) and (-247, 2126) at river level). - **The station pontoon.** For 1967 it should be a roofed wooden pontoon, not today's open concrete one.
- **Moorings density.** No source gave 1967 mooring numbers. The game's 42% sailing / 17% motor retention is a judgement, not a sourced figure.

- Britannia Royal Naval College's Sandquay jetties and pontoons (floating at (-341, 1322), (-330, 1276), (-457, 1407), by "Hindostan"): no dates found. Some college boat landing very likely existed in 1967 (inference).

## 4. Roads and land: North Embankment and Coronation Park reclamation, Mayor's Avenue, A379/A3122, Townstal

### Takeaway
The North Embankment and Coronation Park were made long before 1967. The North and South Embankments opened in 1885, the North Embankment was extended in 1930, and the Coronation Park reclamation was completed in 1937, so the park's land is correct for 1967. The edge, though, was about 6 m narrower and lower before the 1985–1990 works. No dated road changes were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Embankments.** The North and South Embankments opened in 1885. Boatbuilding yards on the south side of Coombe Mud, at the foot of Ridge Hill, lasted until the North Embankment was extended north in 1930. Coronation Park was created by reclaiming the mud behind it, completed in 1937 ([Wikipedia, Dartmouth, Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon); [SW Coast Path town trail](https://www.southwestcoastpath.org.uk/walksdb/452/printable/)).
- **Conflicting dates.** The Devon historic towns survey dates the North Embankment work to 1928–1932 ([Devon Historic Coastal and Market Towns Survey, Dartmouth](https://dartmouthplan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/J3-Devon-Historic-Coastal-and-Market-Towns-Survey.pdf)). A local article says the infill was done in 1928–29 by Welsh miners ([This is my Devon](https://thisismydevon.co.uk/2023/07/08/unravelling-the-story-of-a-ww1-submarine-buried-under-dartmouths-coronation-park/)). All of these predate 1967.
- **Mayor's Avenue.** Shipbuilders on Mayor's Avenue protested in 1885 that the Embankment cut the street off from the river ([Wikipedia, Dartmouth, Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon); [Discover Dartmouth](https://discoverdartmouth.com/history-of-dartmouth)), so the street line is pre-1967.
- **Health centre site.** The health centre stands on part of the park-and-ride overflow car park on the A3122, where it meets the A379 by Lidl ([Torbay and South Devon NHS FT](https://www.torbayandsouthdevon.nhs.uk/about-us/news-and-publications/news/2023/05/new-health-and-wellbeing-centre-opens-in-dartmouth-25855/)).

### Inferences
- **Land.** Coronation Park, the North Embankment road line and Mayor's Avenue are pre-1967 land. Only the waterside strip (about 6 m) and the raised quay edge changed afterwards.
- **Car parks.** The park-and-ride and its overflow (the health centre site) are post-1967 car parks on what was probably farmland at the town's edge. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- When the Townstal road was renumbered B3122 to A3122 (if it was), and any Townstal relief roads or junction changes. No source found.
- When the Mayor's Avenue car park was laid out. Not found.
- Townstal estate roads: undated.

## 6. The game's removal list: verification and corrections

### Takeaway
Most named removals are right or plausible, but there are four corrections:
- The name "Spar" also removes two buildings that are not the Townstal filling station, one of them in the old town centre.
- The kept "Kingswear ferry landing" at (202, 359) is not where the data's own passenger-ferry route lands.
- The kept "Double Steps Pontoon" was rebuilt recently.
- Dart Marina existed in 1967, so its pontoons should not be described as removed (and, as it happens, they are not).

### Cited Findings
- **Entries checked against the sources above:**
  - **Correct, dated:** Dart Lifeboat Station (2007), The Flavel (2005), Dartmouth Health and Wellbeing Centre (2023), Kings Quay House (1975), Noss on Dart Marina and its pontoons (marina after 1999; present one 2023).
  - **Correct but undated:** Sainsbury's, Lidl (existed by 2017), Dartmouth Leisure Centre, the park-and-ride toilets, BP. Sources are in the table under question 1.
  - **Probably correct:** Marina View Apartments (leases 1990/1995; [Waterside Properties](https://www.watersideproperties.com/property/marina-view-kingswear-devon/); [The Coastal House](https://thecoastalhouse.co.uk/property/3-marina-view-kingswear/)).
  - **Undated:** Clarence House, Dartside Court, Lee Court, Mayflower Court, Sails, Vavasour House, the Sewage Pumping Station, the toilet and shower block, and the community greenhouse.
- **Darthaven (Kingswear):** post-1967, founded in the 1970s ([Darthaven Yearbook 2024](https://www.darthaven.co.uk/userfiles/downloads/DarthavenYearbook2024MASTER.pdf)). The removal is correct. The marina's lettering being dropped is also correct.
- **Dart Marina (Sandquay):** a marina existed from 1965 ([DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/a-brief-history-of-dartmouth/); [Dart Marina history](https://dartmarina.com/history/)). The SOURCES.md line "The marinas date from the 1970s onwards" is wrong for Dart Marina.
- **Newcomen Engine (kept by the game):** correct to keep, erected 1963/64 ([Devon Gardens Trust](https://devongardenstrust.org.uk/gardens/royal-avenue-gardens); [Visit South Devon](https://www.visitsouthdevon.co.uk/things-to-do/the-newcomen-engine-p1529933)).
- **Coronation Park and the North Embankment land (kept):** correct, reclaimed by 1937 ([Wikipedia, Dartmouth, Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon)).
- **Double Steps Pontoon (kept by the game as a ferry landing):** a new pontoon at Double Steps was reported c.2023 ([Kingsbridge Today](https://www.kingsbridge-today.co.uk/news/new-pontoon-626136)). Its present form is post-1967.

### Inferences
The following come from inspecting `public/geo/features.json` and `era1967.ts`, not from web sources.
- **"Spar" matches three buildings.**
  - (-1928, -32): the Townstal BP filling-station shop. Removing it is correct.
  - (-1444, 505): beside "Crescent Court" and near St Clement's, Townstal.
  - (-496, 366): in the old town centre, next to "The Windjammer" (-505, 362) and "Greek Delight" (-495, 388), probably Victoria Road. It is almost certainly a historic building with a modern shopfront.
  - Recommendation: remove only the Spar at (-1928, -32), by position, not by name.
- **`KINGSWEAR_FERRY = { e: 202, n: 359 }` is not the passenger ferry's landing.**
  - The data's "Dartmouth Pedestrian Ferry" route runs from (-24, 80), beside Kingswear station at (37, 71), to (-243, 349). Its Kingswear end is the fixed structure at (-13, 74), which is kept anyway because it is not floating.
  - The pier at (202, 359) is a 16 m fixed gangway with a floating T-head on the Kingswear shore between Darthaven and the Hoodown depot. It is probably a Darthaven boatyard pontoon (unverified). For 1967 it should be removed, not kept.
- **Dart Marina's pontoons are kept by the code.** The non-floating piers around (-211…-285, 1068…1232) pass the filter, so in practice the code agrees with the 1965 marina while the docstring and SOURCES.md say they are removed. Either restate this as intentional or shrink them to a 1960s size.
- **The floating-pontoon rule also removes the BRNC Sandquay pontoons** near "Hindostan" (-366, 1333). Some college landing probably existed in 1967 (unverified).
- **Riverboat Pontoon (-154, 677), kept:** no date found. The Embankment's 1967 river-boat landing was the station pontoon (covered, wooden).
- **Other named buildings worth checking against a 1960s OS map** (possible post-1967 blocks, no evidence either way): "Ford Bank Apartments" (-677, 430), "Crescent Court" (-1445, 488), "Dartmouth Court" (-304, 211), "Kingswear Court" (944, -555), "Marks & Spencer" / Mayors Court (-375, 553), the unnamed Dartmouth House on Mayor's Avenue, and the Dart Marina Apartments at Sandquay (unnamed in the data).
- **Greenway Halt** in the railway data (19, 4267) is probably post-1967 (believed 2012, unverified).

### Gaps
- None of the North Embankment blocks except Kings Quay House could be dated. An OS 1:2500 sheet of the late 1960s (NLS) would settle all seven at once.
- Exact OSM tags of the pier at (202, 359) and of the three "Spar" buildings were not checked against OpenStreetMap itself, only against the game's extract.
- Whether the Dart Marina pontoons are truly non-floating in OSM, or just tagged that way in the extract.
