## 1. Ferries in 1967: Higher Ferry, Lower Ferry, the passenger ferry, the Castle Ferry and the Dittisham ferry

### Takeaway
In June 1967 the river had the following crossings:
- **The Higher Ferry** was the 1960 floating bridge "No. 7". She was diesel-electric and paddle-driven, guided by two cables, and carried 15–18 cars. Philip & Son probably built her.
- **The Lower Ferry** was council-run and used open car floats pushed by brand-new diesel tugs, *Hauley IV* (1965) and *Hauley V* (1966), both from Philip & Son.
- **The passenger ferry** belonged to British Railways and used the 1957 Bideford-built sisters *Adrian Gilbert* and *Humphrey Gilbert*.
- **The Castle Ferry and the Dittisham–Greenway ferry** existed in some form, but nothing found describes their 1960s boats.

### Cited Findings
**Higher Ferry (floating bridge, Sandquay/Dartmouth to Noss/Kingswear side)**
- [V] The floating bridge was authorised by Act of Parliament in 1830. It was suspended in 1855 because it was losing money and reinstated in 1856 with a new ferry — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Higher Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry)
- [V] Horses turned a treadmill winch until 1867, when Philip and Son of Dartmouth built a new steam-powered floating bridge. The service was abandoned in 1908 and sold in 1912 to the owner of the Torpoint chain ferry — [dkfb.co.uk (operator's history)](https://dkfb.co.uk/?p=45)
- [V] The operator's history page credits Philip & Son with the 1896 and 1918 vessels — [dkfb.co.uk History](https://dkfb.co.uk/about/history/)
- [V2] **The vessel in service in 1967 was "No. 7", built in 1960** — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Higher Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry); [Flickr, JHLuxton "FERRY NUMBER 7"](https://www.flickr.com/photos/jhluxton/51001102976)
- [C] **Capacity:** Wikipedia says No. 7 carried up to 18 cars ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry)). The operator's history says she was designed for 15 cars ([dkfb.co.uk History](https://dkfb.co.uk/about/history/)).
- [V] **Propulsion:** No. 7 was **diesel-electric**. A 165 hp, 6-cylinder Dorman engine drove a 400-volt generator, which powered electric motors turning the paddles — [dkfb.co.uk History](https://dkfb.co.uk/about/history/)
- [V2] **She was guided by the cables but propelled by paddle wheels**, not hauled along the cables — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry); [Bridges of Britain blog](https://bridgesofbritain.wordpress.com/2016/06/18/dartmouth-higher-ferry/)
- [C] **Builder:** a Flickr caption says No. 7 was built in 1960 at the nearby Philip & Son yard ([Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/photos/jhluxton/51001102976)). A Kingswear Castle Trust article says her paddle-wheel design is "very similar to that of KC [Kingswear Castle], since both were built at the same local yard" ([kingswearcastle.org, Dec 2007 picture of the month](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/?p=17274); possibly the [Sep 2004 entry](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/?p=17729)). The operator's page does not name a builder for the 1960 vessel ([dkfb.co.uk History](https://dkfb.co.uk/about/history/)).
- [V] No. 7 ran for about 40 years and was replaced in late June 2009 by a 36-car ferry (*Pendennis*) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry); [ship-rex postcard listing "Dartmouth Higher Ferry (1960)"](https://www.ship-rex.com/dartmouth-higher-ferry-1960a-67195-p.asp)
- [V] Today the landings are on the Dartmouth side near the Dart Marina Hotel and on the Kingswear side near the Floating Bridge Inn. The present operator is the Dartmouth–Kingswear Floating Bridge Company — [BFI Player record](https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/film/watch-south-devon-and-the-dartmouth-higher-ferry-1964-online)
- [V] **Period film reference:** *South Devon and the Dartmouth Higher Ferry* (1964) is a 7-minute silent amateur film by Eric Lynex, held by the South West Film & Television Archive at The Box, Plymouth, and free on BFI Player. It is the best moving-image source for No. 7's appearance three years before 1967 — [BFI Player](https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/film/watch-south-devon-and-the-dartmouth-higher-ferry-1964-online)
- [V] There is a photo of the predecessor floating bridge: Francis Frith, "Dartmouth, The Floating Bridge c.1950" — [Francis Frith](https://www.francisfrith.com/dartmouth/dartmouth-the-floating-bridge-c1950_d7010); [Dartmouth Museum catalogue (c.1950, cars disembarking)](https://www.dartmouthmuseum.org/directory/floating-bridge-dartmouth/)

**Lower Ferry (car floats pushed by tugs, Dartmouth (Bayard's Cove/Lower Ferry slip) to Kingswear)**
- [V] Earlier history: a rowing boat worked from Kingswear and a "horse boat" carried horses and carts. A steam ferry was added in April 1867. The rowing boat finally stopped in 1915, when the *Relief* came in — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Lower Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Lower_Ferry)
- [V] A tug named *Hauley* was in use from about 1914 and was later converted to a motor tug. *Hauley I* and *Hauley II* were delivered in 1930 and 1932. The lessee, General Estates, acquired *Hauley III* in 1933 — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Lower Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Lower_Ferry)
- [V] In the 1920s the railway sold the ferry rights to Dartmouth Corporation. General Estates surrendered its lease back to Dartmouth Council in 1946, and the council reluctantly ran the ferry itself, though it then made a profit. The ferry passed to South Hams District Council in 1974 — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Lower Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Lower_Ferry)
- [V] **Two new diesel tugs, *Hauley IV* and *Hauley V*, came from "Philips" (Philip & Son) in 1965 and 1966.** That makes both of them brand new in June 1967 — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Lower Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Lower_Ferry)
- [V] Today's floats are *Tom Casey* (1989) and *Tom Avis* (1994), pushed by *Hauley IV*, *V* and *VI*, so **neither present float existed in 1967**. Wikipedia photo captions mention launches of a "Float II" and a "Float III", with no dates given — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Lower Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Lower_Ferry)
- [V] *Hauley VI* recently finished a year-long refit. The ferry works as a floating platform pushed along by a tug — [news report via stevenrlarson.ghost.is](https://stevenrlarson.ghost.is/dartmouth-ferry-tug-is-fit-for-service-again-after-year-long-refit/)
- [V] Postcard and photo references for later visual checks: Simplon "Devon Ferry & Excursion Ship Postcards" (Dart car ferries) — [simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html](https://simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html); a modern photo of *Hauley IV* — [Flickr, guyfogwill](https://www.flickr.com/photos/guyfogwill/51679220258); the DHRG book on Dart crossings — [A River to Cross, Dartmouth History Research Group](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-books/a-river-to-cross/)

**Dartmouth–Kingswear passenger ferry (British Railways)**
- [V] The twin-screw steamer *The Mew* replaced the paddle steamer on 31 May 1908 — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)
- [V] In 1954 British Railways decided that *The Mew* was too expensive to run and planned a smaller, cheaper motor vessel — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)
- [V2] **The pair were *Adrian Gilbert* and *Humphrey Gilbert*, built by M. W. Blackmore & Sons of Bideford.** They entered Dartmouth–Kingswear service on 18 March 1957, replacing the temporary ferry *Lady Elizabeth*. They were named after the Elizabethan explorers of Greenway House — [Wikipedia: MV Humphrey Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Humphrey_Gilbert); [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)
- [V] *Adrian Gilbert* was 35 gross tons. She was later *Pride of Falmouth* — [ShipPhotos: Adrian Gilbert](https://www.shipphotos.co.uk/ship/?ship=adriangilbert)
- [C] Build year: a coasters-remembered forum entry labels *Adrian Gilbert* "1950" ([coasters-remembered](https://www.coasters-remembered.com/home/forum/other-vessels-not-only-for-coastal-shipping-enthusiasts/ferries-coastal-passengerships-f-hren-fahrgastschiffe-a-z-aa/ferries-coastal-passengerships-a/5136-adrian-gilbert-imo)). Every other source says 1957.
- [V] In 1972 the ferry went to Dartmouth Borough Council, then to South Hams District Council. Dart Pleasure Craft took it over on 1 January 1977 — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry); [Simplon: Dart Pleasure Craft table](https://simplonpc.co.uk/Dart_Pleasure_Craft.html)
- [C] Later history of *Humphrey Gilbert*: Wikipedia says Millbrook Steamboat & Trading bought her in 1978 and renamed her *Edgcumbe Belle* for Plymouth–Drake's Island. Simplon and Boatshed say she rejoined *Adrian Gilbert* on the Dart ferry — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Humphrey_Gilbert); [Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/Dart_Pleasure_Craft.html); [Boatshed](https://bahamas.boatshed.com/classic-ferry-for-sale-and-still-mca-certified-blog-10004.html). This does not affect 1967.
- [V] Photo leads: Poole Maritime Trust holds an undated photograph of "Dart ferry *Adrian Gilbert*" (Tom Lee/Cyril Perrier) — [Poole Maritime Trust](https://www.poolemaritimetrust.org/?p=13190). Simplon's BR Devon page carries a "Photo of *Adrian Gilbert* in BR service" — [simplonpc.co.uk/BR_Devon.html](https://simplonpc.co.uk/BR_Devon.html)
- [V] Livery context, **not specific to the Dart**: British Railways' Isle of Wight ferries kept the old BR livery until 1964: black hull, white upperworks, buff funnel with black top. From 1965 they changed to a "greeny/blue" hull, white upperworks and a light red funnel with black top (Sealink era) — [kingswearcastle.org, Dec 2023 picture of the month](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/potm/december-2023-ryde-sandown-1964/)

**Castle Ferry**
- [V] Today a Castle Ferry runs from Dartmouth South Embankment to Stumpy Steps at Dartmouth Castle, from Easter to the end of October — [Dart Harbour: ferries](https://www.dartharbour.org/harbour-river-guide/ferries/). No 1960s source was found.

- [V] Frith has photos of Greenway Quay c.1960 — [d34010](https://www.francisfrith.com/dittisham/dittisham-greenway-quay-c1960_d34010), [d34009](https://www.francisfrith.com/dittisham/dittisham-greenway-quay-c1960_d34009). - [U] By 1967 British Railways' ships were moving to the 1965 Sealink-style livery (blue hull, red funnel with black top and the double-arrow symbol). Whether the small Dart ferries were repainted by June 1967 is **unknown**. The safest choice for a game is the older BR scheme (black or dark hull, white upperworks, buff funnel with black top) *or* the 1965 scheme, kept consistent and flagged as a guess.
- [U] Because No. 7 was paddle-driven, she should show **side paddle boxes** in the model, with the two guide cables rising from the water at each slipway. Her motion should be a slow, steady walk across, not a chain-haul.
- [U] The Lower Ferry tugs, built in 1965–66, would be fresh steel motor tugs lashed alongside the float. They would not look like the steam-era *Hauley*. The 1960s floats were probably smaller than today's *Tom Avis* and *Tom Casey*, so a capacity of roughly 6–8 cars is a placeholder guess.

## 2. River boats and excursion vessels in 1967 (River Dart Steamboat Co. and others)

### Takeaway
By June 1967 **no paddle steamer was in service on the Dart**. *Compton Castle* was withdrawn after 1962 and gone to Kingsbridge by 1964. *Totnes Castle* was withdrawn in 1964 and lay derelict at her moorings until she was towed away (and sank) in November 1967. *Kingswear Castle* was withdrawn in 1965 and laid up in Old Mill Creek. She was sold in spring/June 1967 and towed away in late August 1967.

The River Dart Steamboat Co. (RDSC) ran an **all-motor-vessel fleet**: *Cardiff Castle* (1964), *Conway Castle* (1963), *Dartmouth Castle* (1948), *Berry Castle* (1949) and *Seymour Castle* (1938).

### Cited Findings
**The company**
- [V] The RDSC and its predecessors (Dartmouth Steam Packet Co.; Dartmouth & Torbay Steam Packet Co.) were the major Dart ferry and excursion operators for 120 years, until the company folded in 1976. It took the RDSC name in 1904 — [Wikipedia: River Dart Steamboat Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Dart_Steamboat_Co)
- [V] In 1952 the RDSC was acquired by the Wales-based Evans & Reid Investment Co. That is why its last two new boats (1963, 1964) were named after Welsh castles — [Simplon: River Dart Steamboat Company](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html); [Wikipedia: MV Cardiff Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Cardiff_Castle)
- [V] The pleasure-boat business declined in the late 1960s and early 1970s. After the 1974 season the fleet was laid up for sale; *Dartmouth Castle*'s book value was only £685 — [Wikipedia: MV Dartmouth Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Dartmouth_Castle)
- [V] Overnight, the steamers moored **on the Kingswear side of the river, head up-river** (the current runs south for most of the tide). There was little room between the moorings — [kingswearcastle.org, Oct 2004 "Kingswear Castle on the River Dart in the early 1960s"](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/potm/october-2004-kingswear-castle-on-the-river-dart-in-the-early-1960s/)
- [V] Each of *Kingswear Castle*, *Compton Castle* and *Totnes Castle* carried about 500 passengers, about 1,500 between them — [kingswearcastle.org Oct 2004](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/potm/october-2004-kingswear-castle-on-the-river-dart-in-the-early-1960s/)
- [V] A 1959 Dart steamer timetable is reproduced on the Kingswear Castle site — [kingswearcastle.org, "19th September 1959"](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/?p=25738); [paddlesteamers.org](https://paddlesteamers.org/19th-september-1959-river-dart-paddle-steamers)
- [V, low reliability] Livery: one mirror of the Wikipedia RDSC article says the early steamers had black hulls and cabin sides with a black funnel, and that after incorporation in 1906 the funnel became **yellow with a black top** — [mirror of Wikipedia RDSC article](https://xxg.pages.dev/0xL1JpdmVyX0RhcnRfU3RlYW1ib2F0X0NvX0x0ZA). This was not confirmed from a primary source.

**Paddle steamers: status in June 1967**
- [V2] ***Kingswear Castle*** was built in 1924 by Philip & Son, Dartmouth, and worked the Dart with near-identical sisters *Totnes Castle* and *Compton Castle* "until the 1960s". She was **withdrawn in 1965** (early September, after her last season) and **laid up in Old Mill Creek** — [kingswearcastle.org history](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/history/); [maritime-executive blog](https://maritime-executive.com/blog/sighting-the-paddle-steamer-kingswear-castle-in-dartmouth); [Wikipedia: PS Kingswear Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_Kingswear_Castle)
- [C] **Sale:** one account says the RDSC agreed in **spring 1967** to sell her to the Paddle Steamer Preservation Society (PSPS) for £600. A PSPS page says she was "purchased in **June** for £600". Another PSPS account says the buyer was Paddle Steamer Navigation Ltd, with the Society lending the £600 — [paddlesteamers.org Kingswear Castle](https://paddlesteamers.org/?p=15644); [PSPS chronology](https://paddlesteamers.org/psps-ships/kingswear-castle/chronology/); [paddlesteamers.org "Summer 1967"](https://paddlesteamers.org/peep-1967-2/)
- [V2] **She left Dartmouth under tow in August 1967 and arrived at Binfield on the River Medina, Isle of Wight, on Monday 28 August 1967**, on charter to the Ridett brothers (owners of *Medway Queen*) — [paddlesteamers.org "28th August 1967"](https://paddlesteamers.org/28th-august-1967-kingswear-castle/); [kingswearcastle.org "28th August 1967"](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/?p=25329)
- [V] Simplon's fleet list gives her RDSC ownership as 1924–1967 — [Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html)
- [V] She is the last coal-fired paddle steamer in the UK. Her 1904 engines came from the earlier *Kingswear Castle* — [PSPS: Kingswear Castle](https://paddlesteamers.org/psps-ships/kingswear-castle/)
- [V] ***Totnes Castle* (2)** was built in 1923 by Philip & Son ([Plymouth Divers](https://www.plymouthdivers.org.uk/?p=916)). Simplon gives her RDSC service as 1923–1964 ([Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html)). She needed major spending in 1964, which was refused. When *Cardiff Castle* was delivered in **July 1964**, *Totnes Castle*'s wheelhouse and lifeboat were moved to the new boat and the paddler was laid up — [SHIPS Project: Totnes Castle](https://shipsproject.org/Wrecks/Wk_TotnesCastle.html); [Wikipedia: MV Cardiff Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Cardiff_Castle)
- [V2] **She lay at her moorings on the Dart until 1967.** Ray Demelweek then bought her for scrap and patched her up for towing. On **9 November 1967** the Reynolds tug *Antony* (Plymouth) took her in tow for Sutton Harbour, Plymouth, and she sank in Bigbury Bay. HMS *Gleaner* found the wreck in 1994 — [SHIPS Project](https://shipsproject.org/Wrecks/Wk_TotnesCastle.html); [Plymouth Divers](https://www.plymouthdivers.org.uk/?p=916); [submerged.co.uk](https://www.submerged.co.uk/totnes-castle). [C] The SHIPS Project entry also gives the position as Whitsand Bay, and quoted depths vary (39, 43 and 44 m).
- [C] ***Compton Castle*** was built in 1914 by Cox & Co., Falmouth; one source says 1913. She was **withdrawn after the 1962 season** (the most common date; another source says 1963). She became a tea shop and museum at **Kingsbridge** from 1964; one source says the transfer came two years after 1963. A 1965 newspaper describes her first full season of "retirement" at Kingsbridge. Simplon's list says 1914–1964. She later went to Looe and then Truro — [pscomptoncastle.com](https://pscomptoncastle.com/page01b.html); [South Devon Journal 8 Sept 1965 (via pscomptoncastle.com)](https://pscomptoncastle.com/page13a.html); [Truro Civic Society](https://trurocivicsociety.com/?p=2405); [Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html)
- [V] The motor vessel *Dartmouth Castle* (1948) replaced the paddle steamer *Dartmouth Castle* (2), which had been laid up through the Second World War, was judged beyond economic repair, and was sold in 1947 — [Wikipedia: MV Dartmouth Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Dartmouth_Castle); [Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html)

**Motor vessels (the working RDSC fleet in 1967)**
- [V2] ***Cardiff Castle*** was built in 1964 for the RDSC by Bolson's of Poole. She was a near sister of *Conway Castle* and was built to replace *Totnes Castle*, using a government loan scheme. She was delivered in July 1964. She was sold in 1977 and later ran in River Link colours — [Wikipedia: MV Cardiff Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Cardiff_Castle); [SHIPS Project](https://shipsproject.org/Wrecks/Wk_TotnesCastle.html)
- [V] ***Conway Castle*** was built by Philip & Son, Dartmouth, and launched on **25 March 1963**. She carried 400 passengers and reused the **deck rails and wheelhouse salvaged from PS *Compton Castle***. Simplon gives her RDSC service as 1963–1977. She was later sold for service at Upton-on-Severn (1976 or 1977) — [Wikimedia Commons category "Conway Castle (ship, 1963)" (mirror)](https://download.osmand.net/wiki/Category:Conway_Castle_(ship,_1963)); [Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html); [pscomptoncastle.com](https://pscomptoncastle.com/page03c.html)
- [V] ***Dartmouth Castle* (3)** was built in 1948 by Philip & Son, the first post-war delivery to the RDSC. Simplon gives 250 passengers. She is on the National Historic Ships register — [Wikipedia: MV Dartmouth Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Dartmouth_Castle); [Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html); [National Historic Ships 299](https://nationalhistoricships.org.uk/register/299/dartmouth-castle)
- [V] ***Berry Castle* (3)** was built in 1949 by Philip of Dartmouth, similar to *Dartmouth Castle* (3). Simplon gives 150 passengers. Dart Pleasure Craft renamed her *Totnes Castle* (3) in 1976 — [Simplon RDSC](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html); [Wikipedia: River Dart Steamboat Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Dart_Steamboat_Co)
- [V] ***Seymour Castle*** was built in 1938 by Ferris & Blank of Dartmouth: 60 ft (18 m) overall, twin diesel, 100 passengers. The RDSC owned her 1938–1972 — [Wikipedia: MV Seymour Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Seymour_Castle)
- [V] Another operator: G. H. Riddalls ran "Red Cruisers", with red hulls and white superstructure. The period is unstated — [Simplon: Dart Pleasure Craft table](https://simplonpc.co.uk/Dart_Pleasure_Craft.html)

### Inferences
- [U] **What a player would see in June 1967:**
  - *Kingswear Castle*, laid up and idle (no smoke), moored in **Old Mill Creek** on the Dartmouth side above the town. She had just been sold and was waiting for her tow (she left in August).
  - The hulk of ***Totnes Castle***, rusting and stripped of her wheelhouse and lifeboat, at a river mooring. Exactly where is unknown; a Kingswear-side mooring is plausible given the steamers' usual moorings.
  - Working the Dartmouth–Totnes and harbour cruises: the motor vessels *Cardiff Castle*, *Conway Castle* (both about 400 passengers, three-deck "Castle" river boats), *Dartmouth Castle* (250), *Berry Castle* (150) and *Seymour Castle* (100, 60 ft).
  - In 1967 **neither *Totnes Castle* nor *Compton Castle* should appear under steam**.
- [U] The modern game code already uses a dark blue hull for *Cardiff Castle*, taken from the current River Link livery. Her **1967 RDSC livery is unknown**. If the yellow-funnel-with-black-top tradition held, an RDSC funnel (or a dummy funnel on the motor vessels) in buff/yellow with a black top would be the period-plausible choice. That remains unverified.
- [U] Totnes and the upper river are outside the scope here, but the RDSC boats would pass up-river beyond Dittisham, so they belong on the lower-river traffic paths.

### Gaps
- No 1967 timetable or sailing frequency was found. The landing stages were not confirmed for 1967, though the RDSC almost certainly used the South Embankment pontoon at Dartmouth and Kingswear.
- Hull, superstructure and funnel colours of the 1960s RDSC motor vessels were not found.

## 3. Shipyards: Philip & Son at Sandquay and Noss, other boatbuilders, and the coaling trade

### Takeaway
Philip & Son (Sandquay on the Dartmouth bank, Noss on the Kingswear bank) was **still building small ships in the 1960s**: light vessels in 1960 and 1963, Higher Ferry No. 7 in 1960, *Conway Castle* in 1963, the Lower Ferry tugs *Hauley IV* and *V* in 1965–66, and *Shannon Heather* in 1968, plus tugs and ferries. That contradicts a museum claim that shipbuilding closed in 1964. The coal-bunkering trade was finished or nearly so by 1967. Its main post-war tug, *Portwey*, left in 1951. No source shows coal hulks still working in 1967.

## 4. Naval presence: BRNC boats, the Dartmouth Training Squadron, laid-up ships

### Takeaway
In 1967 the **Dartmouth Training Squadron** (the 17th Frigate Squadron) consisted of the Type 12 (Whitby-class) frigates **HMS *Torquay*** (leader, Captain Ronald Forrest, Aug 1966 – Dec 1967), ***Scarborough***, ***Eastbourne*** and ***Tenby***. They took cadets on Mediterranean, Baltic, Scandinavian and transatlantic cruises in term time. Earlier in the 1960s the squadron used Type 15 frigates such as HMS *Venus*.

The modern "blue-and-white" P1000 picket boats date from **1975**, so they must not appear in 1967. Nothing was found on laid-up merchant ships in the 1960s Dart.

### Cited Findings
- [V] The 17th Frigate Squadron served as the Dartmouth Training Squadron for cadets of Britannia Royal Naval College (BRNC). Its cruises coincided with College terms and usually alternated between the Mediterranean and the Baltic. It was disbanded in 1972 — [Wikipedia: 17th Frigate Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Frigate_Squadron_(United_Kingdom))
- [V] Squadron commanders: Capt. Christopher Wake-Walker in HMS *Tenby* and *Eastbourne*, Dec 1964 – Aug 1966; **Capt. ## 7. Navigation marks, buoys and lights; the harbour authority in 1967

### Takeaway
In 1967 two statutory bodies ran the river:
- **the Dartmouth Harbour Commissioners**, under the Dartmouth Harbour Act 1951;
- **the River Dart Navigation Commissioners**, under the River Dart Navigation Act 1962, for the upper river.

They were merged into the Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority only in 1975–76. The entrance was marked by the unlit 1864 Kingswear Daymark, by fixed leading lights, and by the Blackstone rock. No 1960s source was found for individual buoys (Castle Ledge, Checkstone and others).

### Cited Findings
- [V] The Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority Act 1975 amalgamated the **Dartmouth Harbour Commissioners** (Dartmouth Harbour Act 1951) and the **River Dart Navigation Commissioners** (River Dart Navigation Act 1962) — [Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority Act 1975 (vLex)](https://legislation.vlex.co.uk/vid/808182321)
- [C] Start date of the new authority: its 2013 accounts say it was established by Act on 1 August 1975 ([DHNA Accounts 2013](https://www.dartharbour.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Accounts-2013.pdf)). The authority's own 2026 news item and Wikipedia say it was formed in April 1976 ([DHNA news 2026](https://www.dartharbour.org/news-and-notices/news/2026/04/01/dart-harbour-navigation-authority-celebrates-50-years-of-stewardship-sustainability-and-service-to-the-river-dart/); [Wikipedia: DHNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_Harbour_%26_Navigation_Authority)). Either way, **it did not exist in 1967**.
- [V] Wikipedia says a Dart Harbour Commission was established in the 1880s — [Wikipedia: DHNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_Harbour_%26_Navigation_Authority)
- [V] The **Kingswear Daymark** is an unlit stone tower (station established 1864), now owned by the DHNA. One directory dates it 1865 — [Lighthouse Digest database](https://lhdigest.com/Digest/database/uniquelighthouse.cfm?value=1917); [ibiblio Lighthouses of SW England](https://ibiblio.org/lighthouse/engsw.htm). A Geograph caption calls it a stone tower erected by the harbour authority to show the entrance, "not lit so is of no practical use at night" — [Geograph 4501255](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/4501255)
- [V] Two fixed leading lights, 110 ft (34 m) apart, lead ships to the centre of the entrance channel. The Blackstone rock, 8 ft above high water, is a landmark for avoiding the entrance shoals. No date is given for either — [Wikipedia: Dartmouth Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Harbour)
- [V] The Henrician Kingswear Castle was completed in 1502, and Dartmouth Castle was begun in 1388. Both stand at the entrance as prominent marks — [English Heritage: Dartmouth Castle history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/dartmouth-castle/history/); [Landmark Trust: Kingswear Castle history album](https://cms.landmarktrust.org.uk/globalassets/3.-images-and-documents-to-keep/history-albums/kingswear-castle-history-album.pdf)

### Inferences
- [U] The 1967 entrance would show the Daymark on the Kingswear hill, the two castles, a sector or leading light near Kingswear, and channel buoys at Castle Ledge and the Checkstone. This is general knowledge, **unverified** here, and a likely period-accuracy trap.
