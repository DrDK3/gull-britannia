# Verification pass: town buildings, streets and everyday life, Dartmouth and Kingswear, June 1967

Status of this file: complete for this pass (second save). Baseline is June 1967. Every row says what was actually opened. Sources were read on 19 November 2026.

## How this pass was done, and what could not be opened

- **Read in full (list-entry text):** britishlistedbuildings.co.uk (BLB), which reproduces the official Historic England list-entry text and gives the Historic England "Source ID" (the NHLE number). About 60 Dartmouth and Kingswear entries were opened. List entries are mostly the 1994 resurvey (Dartmouth) and 1985 (Kingswear), so "now" in a list entry means the 1980s-90s, not 1967.
- **Could NOT be opened:** `historicengland.org.uk` and `www.heritagegateway.org.uk` (which redirects to it) answer every request with a Cloudflare "Just a moment..." browser challenge (HTTP 403). `www.francisfrith.com` does the same. `britainfromabove.org.uk` resets the connection. So no Heritage Gateway (Devon HER) record, no Frith caption and no Britain from Above image was read. The Historic England text itself was read via BLB.
- **Read in full:** Dartmouth History Research Group (DHRG) pages, plus three DHRG spreadsheets "Dartmouth Shops & Businesses" North / Central / South (DHRG Nos 103135-103137, compiled c.2015-2021 from directories, Chronicle adverts and memories), the DHRG "Dartmouth Pubs & Inns" PDF (No. 100011) and "Dartmouth Cinema, a brief history" PDF (No. 100022). Cinema Treasures page 29900. Dartmouth Museum "About". The Dartmouth Conservation Area Appraisal PDF (post-2010) and the Devon Historic Coastal and Market Towns Survey: Dartmouth PDF (AC archaeology, April 2016), both text-searched. Wikipedia articles (raw text, read November 2026).
- Dates of sources are given in the tables. "Confirmed" means the full text of an opened page says it.
- Also read: Met Office data files on www.metoffice.gov.uk (regional monthly series, the Chivenor station file, the HadSWEP daily rainfall series and HadCET daily temperatures). The Met Office Monthly Weather Report is on a different host and was not opened. No tide table could be opened.
- The tables below appear in the order 1, 2, 4, 5, 3; the numbers are only labels.

A1. **Dartmouth station building (as it was before restaurant conversion).** Painted timber-framed single-storey building on a brick plinth, six bays, hipped roof; "Canopy with timber valance across the front carried on cast-iron brackets. It is roofed with corrugated-iron and glass"; "Hipped roof has flat top to accommodate ventilators, enclosed by a balustrade of ornamental cast-iron; each standard with a large star and taller, more-elaborate finials each end"; at the south end "a contemporary covered approach originally connecting to the ferry landing stage; a pitched corrugated-iron roof supported at the end on a single upright iron girder". "Old timber thresholds indicate two original front doorways, one in the bay right of centre and another in the right end bay." Before 1986 it stood "partially jettied out over the river on oak piles" (Wikipedia). https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101297055-former-dartmouth-station-dartmouth

A2. **Station jetty and pontoon (c.1889-1986).** Jetty 13 ft 6 in by 78 ft to a covered pontoon 70 ft by 30 ft. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_railway_station

A3. **Butterwalk.** Four houses, Nos 6-12 Duke Street; "an arcade of granite piers with moulded capitals" ("13 granite piers and is now of 11"); carved bressummer; first floor "exposed timber-framing" carved with "strapwork patterns and guilloche"; No. 6 and No. 8 slate-hung above, Nos 10 and 12 "plastered upper floor"; No. 10 has the original first-floor oriel of "8 forward lights" with "brackets carved as animals"; shop fronts "mid C20"; "C20 rendered brick chimneyshafts"; slate roofs. https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197516-the-butterwalk-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197518-the-butterwalk-dartmouth

A4. **Guildhall, Victoria Road.** "Dated 1849. Local limestone rubble with Bathstone dressings; slate roof ... Restrained Tudor style. 2 storeys ... large central Tudor-arch doorway ... Balcony and steps both sides have ornate cast-iron railings ... 2 ventilators with small spires projecting through the ridge". "Guildhall, now municipal offices, originally subscription rooms and school." Exposed stone, in contrast to rendered neighbours (HCMTS). https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101218299-the-guildhall-and-attached-boundary-wall-to-west-dartmouth

A5. **South Embankment "Elizabethan" group of 1888-93** (borough surveyor E.H. Back): York House (dated 1893) "timber-framed show fronts ... grey limestone rubble with red brick and some Bathstone dressings ... tall red brick chimneyshafts in Elizabethan style ... jettied floors, projecting bays, balustraded balconies"; 1 The Quay has framing panels "filled with full-width slates with toothed edges, alternately grey and purple"; the Raleigh (No. 5, "Dated 1888-1889") "painted Flemish-bond red brick ... red brick star-shaped chimneyshaft; steeply-pitched gabled slate roof with pierced crested ridge tiles". https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197512-york-house-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197585-1-the-quay-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197575-raleigh-court-raleigh-court-and-the-raleigh-public-house-the-raleigh-public-house-dartmouth

A6. **Old Post Office, 9 South Embankment.** Three storeys, red brick, Bath stone dressings, rusticated limestone plinth, round-headed ground- and second-floor windows, "Deep moulded stone cornice and low brick parapet"; "Originally there were 2 round-headed front doorways, one each end". https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197576-the-old-post-office-dartmouth

A7. **Bayard's Cove.** No. 3 "Custom House": plastered ground floor, painted brick above, and over the wide double door "a painted board, 'H.M. Customs and Excise', and above it painted relief Royal Arms" (as seen 1994). No. 4 Old Custom House: pedimented centre, segmental door hood, "left lead downpipe is original with winged cherub heads". No. 6: "original cast-iron balcony ornamented with rosettes and anthemia". Nos 11 and 12: "front wall of slate-hung timber-framing above first-floor level" (No. 12's garage door and aluminium picture windows are "late C20", so not 1967). Quay: "old cobbled quayside", "a flight of granite steps down to the estuary opposite No.7". Fort: "Thick rubble wall with a regular series of putlog holes ... Eleven splayed gunports ... Parapet mostly collapsed". https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101208079-custom-house-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197533-quay-walls-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101208168-bearscove-castle-dartmouth

A8. **Lamp posts.** Seven listed "Late C19" cast-iron posts. Boat Float pair: two fluted arms on a foliage standard. Bayards Hill and Bayard's Cove type: "Large circular plinth ... bulbous bottom section, enriched with acanthus leaves, to fluted tapering stem. 4 curving brackets to hold restored lantern with top ventilator". The two on Bayard's Cove were "reset in [their] present position c1975". Smith Street pair carry a "C20 replacement lantern". Market House Inn has a "Very good C19 lamp and bracket fixed to right corner ... carrying an unusually large lantern". https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101293334-lamp-post-approximately-6-metres-to-east-of-no-1-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197534-lamp-post-approximately-04-metres-to-east-of-the-newcomen-road-embankment-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197498-the-market-house-inn-dartmouth

A9. **Market Square.** Horse trough with drinking fountain, "Dated 1898. Granite ashlar", with granite bollards, west of the market; on the Market Court an "Early painted board ... prohibiting the leaving of mules on any public highway, market place or slip ... dated 1839"; market gates "curving down to centre with iron spiked crest and diagonal plank panels". https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101208308-horse-trough-approximately-2-metres-to-west-of-pannier-market-market-square-dartmouth ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101197497-the-market-court-dartmouth

A10. **War memorial, Royal Avenue Gardens.** Granite Latin cross about 5 m tall on a two-stepped octagonal base, applied metal letters (the wedge for post-1945 conflicts is later). https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101449537-dartmouth-war-memorial-cross-dartmouth

A11. **Kingswear.** Station: "Painted horizontally boarded timber frame, and rendered plinth. Corrugated asbestos hipped roof ... Flat roofed wooden canopy over front with moulded cornice, zigzag fascia"; platform canopy "on iron lattice piers". Royal Dart Hotel: "Italianate style stuccoed", three storeys and attic, "Italianate tower on north side" with "clock face" and louvred bell-stage; river front has a "wide splayed two-storey bay with cast iron balcony with tented roof on thin iron columns"; a wing "carried over carriageway ... forming a covered way to the railway station platforms"; "The Hotel was formerly known as the Royal Dart Yacht Club and Family Hotel". Granite milepost on the Lower Ferry slipway with distances "in miles, furlongs and poles". K6 kiosk in The Square. St Thomas: "Short C14 west tower ... with embattled parapet", slate rubble; body 1847 "local limestone rubble with freestone dressings", clock face. Daymark: "Built in 1864. Slate rubble tower 80 feet high. Octagonal on plan, sharply battered with truncated open top, and tall narrow pointed-head openings on each side at base forming eight stilted piers." https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101308783-kingswear-station-kingswear ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101146644-royal-dart-hotel-kingswear ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101108545-mile-post-immediately-south-of-royal-dart-hotel-kingswear ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101108544-church-of-st-thomas-of-canterbury-kingswear ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101108546-the-tower-kingswear

A12. **Station pontoon bridge.** Smart (1999): "In 1928 new pontoon bridges manufactured by John Lysaght & Co. Ltd of Bristol replaced that of 1864 on the Kingswear side and that of 1890 on the Dartmouth side"; they had "low walkways along the sides" for foot passengers because vehicles used them in the days of the steam ferry *The Mew* (withdrawn 8 October 1954). So in 1967 both pontoons are reached by 1928 steel bridges wide enough for a vehicle. The 1957 ferries were "two purpose-built 55' wooden motor boats ... 35 ton Diesel engined craft ... named at a ceremony at Kingswear on 18th March 1957". https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/harbour-papers-part-iv-the-ferries/

A13. **Mayor's Avenue and the North Embankment in 1967 (DHRG, HCMTS).** West side of Mayor's Avenue, in the spreadsheet's house-number order (the direction along the street is not stated): Cundells garage/store at No. 1; an upholsterer at No. 2; THE CINEMA at Zion Place; a Western National bus park where the depot building had been demolished in the 1940s (the site was still called "the former bus depot" when excavated in 1995); the Denning of Dartmouth garage (Vauxhall agent); Hawke & Sons' stone warehouses on the north side (list entry: "Local stone rubble; roof of corrugated asbestos, replacing slate ... paired loading doorways, both containing old plank doors"; "Front end of the warehouse is now behind a C20 building"); a police station ("built 1893"; "new Police Station 1950?"). East side: the Newcomen engine (1963), with no tourist office. North Embankment: the Dartmouth Clinic, the Dartmouth Boatel on the gasworks site, the disused Pinchin Johnson paint works, the St John Ambulance station. https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/dartmouth-shops-businesses-north/ ; https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101253862-warehouse-at-sx-8780-5155-western-warehouse-of-hawke-and-sons-dartmouth

A14. **Duke Street's post-war gap-fill.** Nos 14-24 Duke Street were bombed in 1943 and rebuilt (No. 14 in 1964, No. 18 the Midland Bank in 1953), so the north-west end of Duke Street in 1967 is plain 1950s-60s rebuilding next to the restored Butterwalk. https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/dartmouth-shops-businesses-central/

A15. **Pub signs.** Royal Union Inn (not Dolphin), Market Inn (not Market House Inn), Commercial Hotel (probably; not Windjammer), Castle Hotel / Royal Castle Hotel, Seven Stars, Dartmouth Arms, Seale Arms, Globe Inn (about to close), Raleigh Hotel, Queens Hotel, Harry's Bar. Brewery names recorded: Plymouth Breweries (Royal Union), Symonds (Castle Hotel, from 1939-45), Starkey, Knight and Ford of Paignton (Market Inn, in 1903). https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/dartmouth-pubs-inns/

A16. **High-street multiples present in 1967:** Woolworths (13 Victoria Road, 1956-2001), Home & Colonial Stores (15 Duke Street), Radio Rentals (Foss Street, 1961), Eastman's butchers (22 Duke Street), National Provincial and Midland banks (9 and 18 Duke Street), SWEB showroom/office (25 Fairfax Place, 1969), a Spar grocer in South Ford Road (1960s-70s). International Stores (Fairfax Place 19 and South Embankment 7) is undated. No Lloyds or Barclays branch is recorded for the 1960s in the spreadsheets. https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/dartmouth-shops-businesses-central/

A17. **Headlines by day, 1-10 June 1967** (Wikipedia "June 1967" and "1967 in the United Kingdom", read 2026; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_1967 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_in_the_United_Kingdom). Sat 3 June: Air Ferry crash on the Canigou near Perpignan, "All 83 passengers and five crew" killed, "British vacationers"; Lord Tedder dies. Sun 4 June: Stockport, "killing 72 passengers and crew". Mon 5 June: war begins in the Middle East; the GPO issues the first Machin stamps. Tue 6 June: "The eleven oil-exporting Arab nations announced a halt of shipments to the United States and the United Kingdom"; "Egypt announced the closure of the Suez Canal". Wed 7 June: Israel takes the Old City of Jerusalem; cease-fire with Jordan "At noon". Thu 8 June: Egypt accepts the cease-fire; "A Whiter Shade of Pale" goes to number one. Sat 10 June: "The Six-Day War ended ... cease-fire at 6:00 in the evening". The Sunday papers of 4 June would lead on the Perpignan crash and Monday's on Stockport (inference). A port town would talk about the Suez closure and oil from 6 June (inference).
