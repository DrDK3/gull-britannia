# Everyday life in Dartmouth and Kingswear, June 1967: people, vehicles, street furniture, sounds and conditions

How to read these notes:
- **How the sources were read.** Full-page fetching was blocked for almost every host in this session: Wikipedia, the Met Office, the Official Charts site, the police site, bus photo archives and others. The shared web-search budget then ran out before the last round. Every cited finding below therefore comes from the search engine's extract of the named page, not a full read. Treat page-level detail (exact figures, captions) as needing a spot-check before anything is printed in the game.
- **Tags.** **[V]** means a named source states it, with the year of the fact or source given. **[V?]** means it is stated only by a weak, anecdotal or enthusiast source, or the extract was ambiguous. **[C]** means I computed it myself with PyEphem 4.2.1 (sun and moon) and it is reproducible. **[U]** means unverified (my own background knowledge); [U] items appear only under Inferences or Gaps.
- **Out of scope.** Specific Dartmouth and Kingswear buildings, the railway and river vessels (ferries included) belong to other researchers and are not covered.

## 1. Road vehicles in 1967 Devon: cars, vans, lorries, buses, taxis, motorcycles, number plates and traffic density

### Takeaway
The 1967 road scene is overwhelmingly British-built. BMC (Austin and Morris: Mini, 1100, Minor) and Ford (Cortina, Anglia) dominate, and the Mk2 Cortina was Britain's best-selling car in 1967, briefly taking the top spot from the BMC 1100. Most plates were still black with white or silver characters, and reflective plates were not even optional until 1968. The suffix letter was **E** for new cars registered from 1 January to 31 July 1967, so no "F" plates yet in early June. Buses in Dartmouth were run by **Western National**, a green Tilling-group company with a depot in the town. Torbay next door was **Devon General** territory, with Leyland Atlanteans and open-top "Sea Dogs".

### Cited Findings
**Cars and the market**
- [V, 1967] The Ford Cortina (the Mk2, launched late 1966) was the UK's best-selling car in 1967, ending the Austin/Morris 1100's run. The UK new-car market rose 6% in 1967 to 1,102,266 registrations. — [Best Selling Cars Blog, "UK 1967: Ford Cortina takes the lead"](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1968/01/uk-1967-ford-cortina-takes-the-lead/)
- [V, 1965–66] The BMC 1100 led in 1965 with 157,679 sales (14.3% share) and in 1966 with 151,946 (14.5%). — [Best Selling Cars Blog, UK 1965–1966](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1967/01/uk-1965-1966-austin-morris-11001300-in-charge/)
- [V?, 1964–71] "Between 1964 and 1971 the Austin 1100 or 1300 were Britain's biggest-selling cars every year, apart from 1967 when the Ford Cortina took the top spot." The extract did not make clear which page this came from; it appeared alongside these results. — [Classics World, Austin 1300 Countryman](https://classicsworld.co.uk/news/when-did-you-last-see-a-austin-1300-countryman/); [Retrowow, best-selling cars of the 1960s](https://www.retrowow.co.uk/transport/60s/motoring/best_selling_cars_1960s_uk.php)
- [V, 1967] Great Britain had **14,102,000 licensed motor vehicles** (all types) in the third quarter of 1967, up from 13,286,000 in 1966 (Ministry of Transport, *Highway Statistics 1967*). — [Commercial Motor, 10 May 1968](https://archive.commercialmotor.com/article/10th-may-1968/65/what-are-the-most-recent-figures-available-as-to-t)
- [V, 1967] Rural counties had high vehicle densities in the 1967 vehicle census: 314 licensed vehicles per 1,000 people in Anglesey and 373 in Cardiganshire. These are Welsh counties; no Devon figure was found. — [Hansard written answer, 25 March 1968](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1968/mar/25/road-fund-licences-wales)

**Car colours (BMC)**
- [V, 1958–67] BMC offered **Old English White (code WT-3)** and **Almond Green (GN-37)** from 1958 to 1967, and Austin and Morris shared colours. BMC used prefix codes from about 1960: GR for greys, BU for blues, GN for greens. A 1966 BMC chart's samples were in effect from 1964 to 1967. — [Classic Car Hub, Austin colour codes](https://classiccarhub.co.uk/austin-classic-paint-colour-codes/); [Classic Car Hub, Morris colour codes](https://classiccarhub.co.uk/morris-classic-paint-colour-codes/); [Unique Cars and Parts, 1966 BMC paint chart](https://uniquecarsandparts.com.au/colour_swatch_BMC_1966)
- [V?, 1959–67] The Mk1 Mini (1959–1967) shared an almost identical palette with other BMC cars. The source warns that its swatches are only approximate because of screen calibration and fading. — [Classic Car Hub, Mini paint colours](https://classiccarhub.co.uk/?p=1164)

**Number plates**
- [V, 1967] Suffix **E** covered 1 January to 31 July 1967, and **F** began on 1 August 1967 and ran to 31 July 1968. The changeover moved to August in 1967 because the motor industry lobbied to spread sales across the year. Suffix plates began in 1963 (A). — [CarReg, suffix plates 1963–1983](https://www.carreg.co.uk/suffix-number-plates/); [Bristol Archives, vehicle registration records guide](https://archives.bristol.gov.uk/guides/Bristol%20Archives%20-%20Vehicle%20registration%20records.pdf)
- [V, 1968/1973] Reflective plates (white front, yellow rear, black characters) were **optional from 1968** (British Standard AU 145:1967) and **compulsory for vehicles built from 1 January 1973**. In June 1967 almost all plates were the older black type with white or silver characters. **Conflict:** one source says reflective plates were introduced, optionally, in 1967. — [Wikiwand, British number plates](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/British_number_plates); [NI Statutory Rule 1968/37 citing BS AU 145:1967](https://legislation.gov.uk/nisro/1968/37/pdfs/nisro_19680037_en.pdf); conflicting: [Tractors wiki, UK plates](https://tractors.fandom.com/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_the_UK)
- [V, pre-1974] Devon licensing-office letter codes included **DV, TT, TA, OD, UO** and the single letter **T**. One source puts OD and TA under the Exeter office. In three-letter marks the last two letters gave the office, so a Devon car might read "xDV 123E" or "xOD 456E". — [Primoreg, DV](https://www.primoreg.co.uk/history/registration-code/dv); [Primoreg, TT](https://www.primoreg.co.uk/history/registration-code/tt); [Primoreg, UO](https://www.primoreg.co.uk/history/registration-code/uo); [Old Classic Car, OD](https://www.oldclassiccar.co.uk/registrations/od.htm); [Olav's Plates, 1960s list](https://olavsplates.com/great_britain_slow2.html)

**Post Office vans**
- [V, 1956–1971] The GPO's Morris Minor vans had bodies built to a GPO design by Bonallack and Sons, Duple and Park Royal, and from 1956 used the 948 cc Minor 1000 engine. About 39,417 Series III Minor vans were supplied to the GPO up to October 1969. Van production ended in 1971, when the Post Office bought 2,404. A 1969 Minor 1000 "telephone utility" is documented (WLF 801G). — [British Telephones, GPO vehicles: Minor vans](https://www.britishtelephones.com/vehicles/minorvans/023.htm); [Lightstraw, Royal Mail Morris Minor](https://lightstraw.uk/gpo/do/rm/morris1.html); [Lightstraw, Post Office Telephones Morris](https://lightstraw.uk/ate/tec/pot/morris1.html)
- [V?, 1970s] Post Office Telephones Minor vans appear in a "Golden Yellow" livery in the 1970s. The searches found no colour for 1967 (see Gaps). — [British Telephones, Minor vans gallery](https://www.britishtelephones.com/vehicles/minorvans/126.htm)

**Police and AA vehicles**
- [V, 1965–68] Unit Beat Policing was piloted in 1965 in Kirkby, Lancashire, with Ford Anglia 105E "Panda cars" (the extract describes them as black and white; see Gaps). Bristol bought Morris Minor 1000s in 1968 to introduce the system. — [Panda car (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panda_car); [US Department of Justice NCJRS copy of a 1960s UK study](https://ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Photocopy/29577NCJRS.pdf); [Yahoo/Classic car article, "Morris Minor: the unlikeliest police car"](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/morris-minor-unlikeliest-police-car-151627804.html)
- [V, 1961–68] AA patrolmen stopped saluting members around 1961–62 (the AA says 1962). Austin Mini vans entered AA service from 1962–63 and were standard issue from about 1965. BSA motorcycle-and-sidecar outfits were the core of the fleet into the early 1960s, and sources disagree on when the last ones went: some say 1968. — [Haynes Motor Museum, saluting patrolmen](https://haynesmotormuseum.com/news/are-we-there-yet-saluting-patrolmen); [Lancaster Insurance, "The story of 200 FGN"](https://azuat-bw.lancasterinsurance.co.uk/news/2019/june/10/the-story-of-200-fgn); [Honest John, classic AA vehicles](https://classics.honestjohn.co.uk/best-classics/classic-quirks/classic-aa-vehicles/); [Old Classic Car, AA motorcycles](https://www.oldclassiccar.co.uk/aa-motorcycle.htm)

### Inferences
- **Mix of cars.** In June 1967 a Devon street would mostly show cars from the late 1950s to 1967. Expect many pre-1963 cars with no year letter and suffix plates A to E on newer ones. The Mk2 Cortina (late 1966 on) would be a new, eye-catching car on E or D plates. Morris Minors, Minis, BMC 1100s, Anglias, Mk1 Cortinas, Hillman Minxes and Imps, Vauxhall Victors and Vivas, Triumph Heralds, Austin A35s and A40s, and Land Rovers on farms are all plausible. The BMC and Ford figures are sourced; the rest is [U] background knowledge, so check the model years.
- **How many cars in a street.** At about 14 million vehicles for about 55 million people ([U] population figure), the national density was roughly 250 vehicles per 1,000 people, far below today's. A small-town street in 1967 would look sparse by modern standards: a few kerb-parked cars per block rather than continuous lines. This supports the existing build's choice to halve SailSim's cars. In June the town would gain visitors' cars and caravans ([U]).
- **Colours.** Muted pastels and mid-tones (Old English White, Almond Green, greys, pale blues, beiges), with some two-tone, plus black and maroon on older cars. Treat this as an art-direction inference built on the BMC codes above.
- **Plates.** Every plate should be black with white or silver characters and no reflective plates. New cars registered in early June 1967 would carry E suffixes.
- **Buses.** A Dartmouth scene should show Western National in Tilling green and cream. Ageing Bristol K-type and Lodekka double-deckers, or a Bristol MW or SU single-decker, are all plausible ([U] for the specific types beyond the two photographed Ks). Devon General buses would belong on the Kingswear/Brixham side or in Torbay rather than in Dartmouth itself (see Gaps on Kingswear).

## 2. Street furniture and signage: road signs, kiosks, postboxes, lamps, AA/RAC boxes, petrol pumps and brands, enamel signs, shopfronts, newspapers and posters

### Takeaway
1967 is a **transition year** for signs. The Worboys system (Transport and Calvert lettering, red triangles and circles, blue and green direction signs) became law on 1 January 1965. Regulatory signs were supposed to be converted by the end of 1967, but money was short and conversion was still incomplete in 1970. Fingerposts were unaffected. So a 1967 town would mix new Worboys signs, especially at junctions, with pre-1965 signs (old warning signs with the red ring and triangle, black-and-white fingerposts and the like). Every phone box would be a red **K6**: the K8 did not appear until July 1968. Postboxes carry whatever cipher they were cast with (VR, EVIIR, GR, GVIR or EIIR). Street lighting was moving to concrete columns with orange low-pressure sodium.

### Cited Findings
**Road signs**
- [V, 1961–65] The Worboys Committee was set up in 1961 and reported in 1963 (some sources say 1964). It found existing signs obsolete, and its recommendations became the Traffic Signs Regulations and General Directions, **in force from 1 January 1965**. They brought European-style pictograms and the British-designed "Transport" typeface, and remain the basis of UK signs today. — [Worboys Committee (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worboys_Committee); [SABRE/roads.org.uk, "From War to Worboys"](https://www.roads.org.uk/articles/war-worboys/worboys-report)
- [V, Feb 1967] A minister told Parliament that "the Regulations prescribe that all **regulatory** signs must be changed by the end of this year", adding that new signs "are being provided as quickly as possible within the limits of the funds available". — [Hansard written answer, 1 February 1967, "New signs"](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1967/feb/01/new-signs)
- [V, 1970] In May 1970 an MP still asked why the Worboys report "has not yet been fully implemented". — [Hansard written answer, 28 May 1970](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1970/may/28/worboys-committees-report)
- [V?] Fingerposts "were not affected in 1964" by the changeover. — [SABRE forum](https://www.sabre-roads.org.uk/forum/viewtopic.php?p=1193809)
- [V] A handful of pre-1963 signs still survive today, for example an old-style T-junction sign in Stourport-on-Severn. — [Road signs in the United Kingdom (AARoads wiki)](https://wiki.aaroads.com/wiki/Road_signs_in_the_United_Kingdom); [roads.org.uk, old signs photo gallery](https://www.roads.org.uk/photo/old-signs)

**Telephone kiosks**
- [V, 1935–36] The **K6** was designed by Sir Giles Gilbert Scott and commissioned by the GPO in 1935 for George V's Silver Jubilee. Production began in 1936. It refined his K2 into a smaller, cheaper, more streamlined kiosk, and about 60,000 were installed. — [Historic England, K6 list entry 1420379](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1420379); [The Telephone Box, the story of Kiosk No. 6](https://www.the-telephone-box.co.uk/kiosks/k6); [BT Newsroom, red phone box at 80](https://newsroom.bt.com/bts-classic-british-red-phone-box-rings-up-80-years/)
- [V, 1965–68] The **K8** (Bruce Martin, from a GPO competition in 1965) was **first installed in July 1968**, so none existed in June 1967. About 11,000 were installed by 1983–84. — [BT Archives, "The development of the K8 kiosk"](https://www.bt.com/bt-plc/assets/documents/about-bt/our-history/bt-archives/information-sheets-and-timelines/the-development-of-the-k8-kiosk.pdf); [Historic England, K8 list entry 1493231](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1493231)

**Postboxes**
- [V] Royal ciphers in sequence: **VR** (1837–1901), **EVIIR** (1901–10), **GR** (1910–36), **EVIIIR** (1936, very rare), **GVIR** (1936–52), **EIIR** (1952 on). Scottish boxes carry the Scottish crown rather than EIIR, which is irrelevant in Devon. The Victorian hexagonal **Penfold** pillar box was made 1866–79, in thirteen variants. The Letter Box Study Group counts around 800 UK box types. — [Letter Box Study Group, ciphers](https://lbsg.org/about-boxes/ciphers); [LBSG, kinds of letter box](https://lbsg.org/about-boxes/); [Colne Valley Postal History Museum, history of the pillar box](https://cvphm.org.uk/history.html); [Postal Museum, royal cyphers article](https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/?p=66008)

**Street lighting**
- [V?, 1960s] Concrete columns and brackets (makers such as Concrete Utilities and Stanton) were the norm from the 1960s to the 1980s. Early-1960s lanterns took 140 W SO/H low-pressure sodium lamps, the precursor of the 90 W SOX. Orange low-pressure sodium lighting dominated Britain's major roads from the 1950s. — [English Street Lights Online, survivors](https://streetlightonline.co.uk/Survivors/GRLLAM3.htm); [SABRE wiki, SOX](https://www.sabre-roads.org.uk/wiki/index.php?title=SOX); [Sunday in Old Money blog, "Goodbye sodium" (2023)](https://sundayinoldmoney.blogspot.com/2023/02/goodbye-sodium.html)

**AA boxes**
- [V] The AA's roadside sentry boxes and telephone boxes were features of the period; only about 19 of the phone boxes survive. — [Haynes Motor Museum](https://haynesmotormuseum.com/news/are-we-there-yet-saluting-patrolmen); [Honest John, classic AA vehicles](https://classics.honestjohn.co.uk/best-classics/classic-quirks/classic-aa-vehicles/)

**Petrol brands and forecourts**
- [V?, early 1960s] **National Benzole** rebranded as "**National**" in the early 1960s, with the winged Mercury-head emblem in blue and yellow. — [National Benzole (Wikipedia)](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Benzole); [Sunday in Old Money blog (2017)](https://sundayinoldmoney.blogspot.com/2017/05)
- [V, Aug 1967] **Regent** ("née Trinidad Oil, once half-British") was by 1967 owned by the American **Caltex**. A 1960s-era blogger remembers Regent "already on the way out" during the decade. — [Motor Sport, August 1967, "Petrol"](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/august-1967/11/petrol/); [Sunday in Old Money blog](https://sundayinoldmoney.blogspot.com/2017/05)
- [V] Anglo-American Oil renamed itself **Esso** in 1951. Its **Cleveland** stations kept their own brand until they were rebranded Esso in the early 1970s, so Cleveland was still on forecourts in 1967. **Fina** (Belgian-owned) sold Fina and Super Fina from 1953. **Shell-Mex and BP** marketed Shell and BP in the UK. — [Alamy caption, Cleveland and Esso history](https://www.alamy.es/1954-historical-exterior-view-of-an-ancient-elizabethan-building-essex-england-uk-with-a-large-forecourt-and-petrol-pumps-fuels-sold-included-clevecol-and-aero-with-a-sign-for-cleveland-petrols-cleveland-was-a-british-company-originally-established-in-1920-at-trafford-park-in-manchester-by-norman-davis-which-in-1938-was-majority-purchased-by-the-us-business-the-anglo-american-oil-co-in-1951-anglo-american-began-calling-itself-esso-after-its-orgins-as-standard-oil-eastern-seaboard-standard-oil-in-the-early-1970s-all-cleveland-filling-stations-were-rebranded-esso-image432891503.html); [Motor Sport, February 1953, branded petrol](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/february-1953/22/enthusiasts-directory-no-5-branded-petrol/); [Grace's Guide, Shell-Mex and B.P.](https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/Shell-Mex_and_B._P.)
- [V?] One 1960s motorist saw his first **Gulf** station "around 1968", which suggests Gulf was rare in 1967. VIP was a lesser brand. Independent garages often sold several brands from separate pumps (BP, Shell, National Benzole). — [Sunday in Old Money blog](https://sundayinoldmoney.blogspot.com/2017/05); [Old Classic Car, petrol](https://www.oldclassiccar.co.uk/petrol.htm)

### Inferences
- **Signs on screen.** The game can credibly show both sign generations side by side. Use new Worboys regulatory signs (No Entry, No Waiting, Give Way triangle, 30 mph roundel) at main junctions and on the Embankment. Keep older pre-Worboys warning signs (a triangle on a pole above a symbol plate) and black-on-white fingerposts with cast lettering, which had no deadline. Regulatory signs were legally due for conversion by 31 December 1967, so in June 1967 a few old regulatory signs could still stand.
- **Phone boxes.** Every kiosk should be a red K6 with "TELEPHONE" on illuminated fascia panels and a crown. No K8s.
- **Postboxes.** A historic town like Dartmouth would plausibly have a mix of VR, GR and GVIR boxes, with EIIR on boxes installed after 1952. Wall boxes in walls are plausible too.
- **Street lamps.** On narrow old streets, wall-bracket lanterns and converted older posts are as likely as new concrete columns ([U]). Use orange sodium light at night on main roads, perhaps whiter mercury or tungsten on side streets ([U]).
- **Petrol.** Plausible 1967 forecourt brands in Devon: Shell, BP, Esso, National, Cleveland, Regent, Fina and Mobil ([U] for Mobil). Pumps would be tall cabinet pumps with illuminated globes or panels, served by an attendant ([U]: self-service was rare before about 1970).

## 3. People and dress in summer 1967: holidaymakers, locals, fishermen, shop workers, naval cadets and officers, police, postmen, children and hairstyles

### Takeaway
Provincial Britain in mid-1967 was mostly conventional in dress. London fashion "took time to reach the whole country". Older people dressed much as in the 1950s: jackets and ties, skirts below the knee, hats for older women. The young wore mod-influenced short skirts and shift dresses, and from the summer of 1967 longer hair, beads and kaftans were spreading among mainstream youth. On 1 June 1967 the county force became the new **Devon and Cornwall Constabulary** (sources conflict between 1 April and 1 June). Royal Navy ratings had worn white-topped caps all year since 1956. Midshipmen wear white collar patches, an old distinction.

### Cited Findings
**Police**
- [V, 1967; date conflict] The Devon and Exeter, Cornwall County and Plymouth City forces amalgamated as the **Devon and Cornwall Constabulary** on **1 June 1967**, according to the force's own history and its 50th-anniversary press coverage. **Conflict:** Wikipedia and its mirrors give **1 April 1967**. ## 5. Conditions for 1–10 June 1967: weather, tides, sunrise and sunset, British Summer Time

### Takeaway
Clocks were on **British Summer Time (GMT+1)** from 19 March to 29 October 1967. Sun times are computed precisely below: sunrise about 05:00 BST, sunset about 21:25 BST, civil twilight lasting past 22:00, and the midday sun about 62° high, with solar noon around 13:12 BST. The Moon was at **last quarter on 31 May**, **new on 8 June 1967 (06:13 BST)**, and furthest from Earth on about 2–3 June. So the first days of June had **neap tides, unusually weak because of the distant Moon**, building to **spring tides around 9–11 June**. No June 1967 weather record for South Devon could be reached; the Met Office Monthly Weather Report is the source to obtain.

### Cited Findings
**British Summer Time**
- [V, 1964 order / 1967 debate] Summer time in 1967 ran from **19 March to 29 October**: the 1964 Summer Time Order set the period, and a July 1967 Commons debate confirmed it "ends this year on 29th October". — [Hansard, 14 July 1964, Summer Time](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1964/jul/14/summer-time); [Hansard, 21 July 1967, Summer Time](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1967/jul/21/summer-time)

### Inferences
- **Light and shadows.** These are long June days. In the game's Mission 1 (13:20 BST, 3 June) the sun is at its highest of the day, about 62° up and almost due south (solar noon 13:12 BST), so shadows are short. There is usable light from about 04:20 to 22:00 BST. A thin waning crescent Moon rises about 03:00 BST and is up in daylight. Around 7–9 June nights are moonless, which is useful for night missions.
- **Tide state: neaps then springs.** Neaps fall about 1–2 days after last quarter (31 May), i.e. around **1–2 June**, and the near-apogee Moon would make them among the weakest of the year. Springs fall about 1–2 days after new moon (8 June), i.e. around **9–11 June**, and would be moderate rather than extreme because perigee is not until about mid-June. The "1–2 day age of the tide" for UK waters is [U].
- **Tide height in the game.** On 3 June high water would be near or below mean high water neaps, not near springs. The game's current 4.7 m above chart datum for 13:05 BST on 3 June (from `SOURCES.md`) is probably too high. Something nearer MHWN, about 4 m on Devonport-type levels and less if Dartmouth's datum figures are lower ([U]), would be more plausible. The tidal range that day would be small, roughly 2 m rather than the ~4.7 m spring range.
- **Tide time in the game.** High water roughly equals the Moon's transit plus the port's lunitidal interval. On 3 June the Greenwich transit was 08:23 GMT. With a Dartmouth interval of about 4½ to 6 hours ([U]; derived only loosely from "HW Dover −05:10"), high water would fall around 13:00–15:30 BST, with the other high water about 12h25m earlier or later. The game's 13:05 BST HW is therefore at the early end of plausible. At 13:20 BST the tide is most likely near high water or still rising. This is not a prediction; see Gaps for how to get the real one.
- **Weather.** Without the Monthly Weather Report, "changeable early-summer weather with sunny spells and showers" is a safe default, but it is not verified. CET evidence shows June 1967 was not exceptionally warm, being cooler than June 1966.

## 6. Money and prices, newspaper headlines in early June 1967, and notable events

### Takeaway
Money was pre-decimal: 12 pence (d) to the shilling (s) and 20 shillings to the pound. In 1967 a pint of beer was about 2s 6d, a pint of milk 10d, a large wrapped loaf 1s 2½d and the *Daily Mirror* 4d. A bag of chips was about **a shilling**, a weak but consistent figure. The first ten days of June 1967 were dominated by the Middle East:
- 5 June: the **Six-Day War** begins.
- 10 June: it ends.

British news also carried two air crashes:
- 3 June: the Air Ferry DC-4 near Perpignan.
- 4 June: the **Stockport air disaster**, 72 dead.

In the region, **Francis Chichester** had landed at Plymouth on 28 May to an enormous crowd, and the new **Devon and Cornwall Constabulary** was formed, probably on 1 June. The **Spring Bank Holiday** fell on 29 May 1967, just before the game's dates.

**Holidays and local events**
- [V, 1965–66 announcements] The 1967 **spring bank holiday** was fixed for **Monday 29 May**, the last Monday in May, during the 1965–70 trial that replaced Whit Monday; the 1967 late summer holiday was 28 August. **Conflict:** a 1988 Lords debate dates the trial 1967–71. — [Hansard, 3 June 1965, bank holidays 1967 and 1968](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1965/jun/03/bank-holidays-1967-and-1968); [Hansard, 25 October 1966, bank holidays](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1966/oct/25/bank-holidays); [Lords debate, 7 June 1988](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1988/jun/07/spring-bank-holiday-timing)
- [V] The **Port of Dartmouth Royal Regatta** was first recorded in 1822 and formally established in 1834; Queen Victoria granted the "Royal" title after her 1856 visit. It is a late-August event, so outside the 1–10 June window. — [Dartmouth Regatta, history](https://www.dartmouthregatta.co.uk/history/); [Port of Dartmouth Royal Regatta (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Port_of_Dartmouth_Royal_Regatta)

### Inferences
- **Price list for shop signs and dialogue:**
  - Bag of chips: about 1s (writers could use 9d to 1s).
  - Pasty: about 1s to 1s 6d ([U]; no source found).
  - Daily paper: 4d to 5d.
  - Pint of beer: 2s 6d.
  - Pint of milk: 10d.
  - Large loaf: 1s 2½d.

  Write prices as "1/-" (one shilling), "2/6" (two and six) and "10d". The halfpenny (½d) was still in use. The threepenny bit, sixpence, shilling, florin (2s) and half-crown (2s 6d) are [U] but well-established common knowledge.
- **Newsstand placards for 1–10 June 1967:**
  - 1–4 June: Chichester's homecoming and knighthood; *Sgt. Pepper*.
  - 4–5 June: the Stockport crash.
  - 5–10 June: Middle East war headlines ("ISRAEL AT WAR", "SINAI", "JERUSALEM", "CEASEFIRE").
  - Locally: the new Devon and Cornwall police force.

  Headline wording is illustrative, not quoted.
- **Visitor season.** With the spring bank holiday on 29 May, early June would sit in a lull between the bank holiday crowds and the July–August peak ([U]). Schools were in session, so on weekdays most children in a resort would be locals ([U]).
