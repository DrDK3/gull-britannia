## 1. Kingswear village in 1967: buildings, waterfront and sites

### Takeaway
In June 1967 the Kingswear waterfront was a working British Railways terminus: a rare 100 ft wooden train shed, a canopied long platform, a signal box still in use (closed October 1968), a goods yard and quayside sidings whose traffic had stopped in 1964-65, and the BR passenger-ferry pontoon beside the station. The Royal Dart Hotel (c.1850) stood between station and ferry slip. Darthaven Marina and the Hoodown harbour depot did not exist: the marina company dates from 1975 and Hoodown was built in the mid-1980s on a former wartime Free French base. Kingswear Castle was a private house.

### Cited Findings

**Kingswear station (buildings, platforms, shed, canopy)**
- **Verified (1864).** The Dartmouth and Torbay Railway opened to Kingswear on 16 August 1864. Source: [Historic England list entry 1308783 / search extract](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1308783); [Wikipedia, Dartmouth and Torbay Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_and_Torbay_Railway)
- **Verified (listed 1985; describes the surviving fabric).** Kingswear Station is Grade II listed, first listed 21 May 1985. The list entry describes a rectangular station building housing the booking office, waiting room and offices, with rendered chimney stacks on the ridge. Behind it the platforms stand under a train shed, and one long platform runs north-east with a canopy. Source: [Historic England 1308783](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1308783); [British Listed Buildings 101308783](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101308783-kingswear-station-kingswear)
- **Verified (construction).** The train shed is timber: boarded sides, timber trusses with iron-rod bracing, clad in corrugated asbestos with a glazed ridge. Wikipedia calls it a "rare wooden 100 feet (30 m) train shed". Source: [Historic England 1308783](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1308783); [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Unverified (1960s).** At least two platform faces: an RMweb poster who checked 1960s photographs says the "Platform 2 starter" signal had no route indicator by then. Great Western Railway Journal No. 25 (Winter 1998) has a track plan; one forum poster says it shows "only one island platform". Source: [RMweb, Kingswear signalling](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/116328-kingswear-signalling/); [RMweb, Kingswear track layout](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/179823-kingswear-track-layout/)
- **Unverified (attribution).** No source found credits Brunel with Kingswear's shed. Brunel-era material in search results concerns the South Devon Railway elsewhere (e.g. Totnes). Source: [search extract on Kingswear shed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)

**Signal box**
- **Verified (1894 to 1968).** The Kingswear signal box opened in 1894. It was given a new lever frame in 1960 and closed in October 1968; the following month closure of the whole line from Paignton was formally proposed. So in June 1967 the box was open and in BR use. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Conflict (wording).** One extract says the box "was in use until British Rail services ceased in 1968". BR services in fact ran until late 1972 (see section 2), so that wording conflates the box's closure with the end of BR service. Source: [search extract, Kingswear station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Verified (later).** The box was "closed again in 1991" when control of the whole line passed to a panel at Britannia Crossing, Kingswear. This implies the Dart Valley Railway brought it back into use after 1973. Source: [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Steam Railway (search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway)
- **Unverified (1961 diagram).** A 1961 Signalling Record Society diagram of Kingswear (S918) exists and shows at least one ground-mounted disc signal. I could not open it. Source: [RMweb, Kingswear signalling (2016)](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/116328-kingswear-signalling/); [RMweb, Kingswear track layout (2023)](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/179823-kingswear-track-layout/)

**Goods yard, wharf, sidings, coal**
- **Verified (1866).** Goods trains ran to Kingswear from 2 April 1866. They mattered partly because they carried coal landed at Kingswear. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Verified (1964-65).** The wharf closed to freight on 4 May 1964, and ordinary goods traffic was withdrawn on 14 June 1965. So by June 1967 Kingswear had no goods service. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Verified (today).** The old goods yard between the station and the waterfront is now a car park and boat storage. Some sidings survive opposite the platform and along the line towards Britannia Crossing. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Unverified (later, undated).** The quayside tracks were removed at some later date; the poster gives no year. Source: [RMweb, Kingswear track layout (2023)](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/179823-kingswear-track-layout/)
- **Unverified.** The branch carried "a lot of inwards coal traffic for Torquay Gasworks" (forum). Source: [RMweb, Kingswear track layout](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/179823-kingswear-track-layout/)
- **Verified (background, pre-1940).** Dartmouth's ship-coaling (bunkering) trade declined from the 1880s: Hingston & Co left in 1886, Portland and Weymouth took trade from 1901, and business dried up further around 1938. Coal hulks (stripped old ships used as floating coal stores) were moored in the river (Royal Museums Greenwich photo, c.1889). I found nothing on hulks or the coal trade at Kingswear in the 1960s. Source: [Portwey steam tug history (engineers.scot, 2021)](https://engineers.scot/news/2021-02-09-portwey-the-history-of-a-west-country-steam-tug); [RMG, Dartmouth and Kingswear](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-1069318)

**Passenger-ferry pontoon**
- **Verified.** The railway ferry made a five-minute crossing from the pontoon beside Kingswear station to the Dartmouth pontoon, where the "station" had a ticket office and porters but no track. The GWR ran the ferry itself from 1901. After 1972 the ferry passed to the Borough Council, until 1974. Source: [Wikipedia, Dartmouth railway station / Dartmouth Passenger Ferry (search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry); [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Verified (existing project notes).** British Railways ran the passenger ferry from 1957 to 1972. Source: SOURCES.md in this repository, citing [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)

**Royal Dart Hotel**
- **Verified (c.1850 building).** An Italianate, stuccoed building of about 1850, Grade II listed (Historic England 1146644, listed 1985). It stands between the station and the ferry slipway. Source: [Historic England 1146644](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1146644); [British Listed Buildings](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101146644-royal-dart-hotel-kingswear)
- **Verified (1866).** The railway company opened the "Yacht Club Hotel" at the southern end of the station in 1866. The listing says the building was formerly the "Royal Dart Yacht Club and Family Hotel"; the yacht club had its rooms there until its own clubhouse opened in 1881. The hotel housed passengers waiting to sail for South Africa and beyond. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station (search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station); [Historic England 1146644](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1146644)
- **Verified (1923).** A 1923 directory names Ernest Charles Way as proprietor. Source: [pubwiki, Royal Dart Hotel](https://pubwiki.co.uk/Devon/Kingswear/RoyalDartHotel.shtml)
- **Verified (later, undated).** The building later fell into disrepair. Its owners, Darthaven Marina, had it refurbished into five apartments and one commercial unit; no date found. Source: [Hydrock project page](https://www.hydrock.com/projects/royal-dart-dartmouth)
- **Unverified.** Its use in 1967. I found no source stating whether it was trading as a hotel or bar in 1967. Source: none found (see Gaps).
- **Verified (street furniture).** A listed milepost stands immediately south of the Royal Dart Hotel. Source: [British Listed Buildings 101108545](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101108545-mile-post-immediately-south-of-royal-dart-hotel-kingswear)

**Royal Dart Yacht Club**
- **Conflict (1866 founding date).** The club's own site says Henry Studdy and friends agreed at the Castle Hotel, Dartmouth, on 8 June 1866 to form the Dart Yacht Club; a Wikipedia mirror gives 3 July 1866. Its first premises were two rooms borrowed from the railway hotel in Kingswear. Source: [Royal Dart YC, About the club](https://www.royaldart.co.uk/about-the-club); [Wikipedia, Royal Dart Yacht Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dart_Yacht_Club)
- **Verified.** "Royal" title 1872 (Dartmouth history book extract); purpose-built clubhouse 1881; freehold bought 1921. The clubhouse was refurbished in 1974 and redeveloped in 1991, so the 1967 clubhouse predates both. Source: [Wikipedia, Royal Dart Yacht Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dart_Yacht_Club); [Issuu, Dartmouth history excerpt](https://issuu.com/accpublishinggroup/docs/dartmouth_lo-res/61)
- **Unverified.** Whether the club had a pontoon or its own moorings in 1967. No source found.

**Hoodown and the Darthaven site**
- **Verified (built mid-1980s).** The Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority's Hoodown facility on the Kingswear bank was built in the mid-1980s on what had been a wartime base used by the Free French. DHNA uses it for river maintenance. So in 1967 Hoodown was the former wartime base site, not a harbour depot. Source: a Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority page quoted in search results. The exact page URL was not returned; the authority's site is [dartharbour.org](https://dartharbour.org/harbour-information/sailing-directions).
- **Verified (1943-44).** The Free French Navy's 23rd MTB Flotilla (Vosper 72 ft boats) was stationed at Kingswear from January 1943 to September 1944, preceded by the 20th Flotilla. The 23rd's eight boats made 451 Channel patrols and sank eight ships. Crews were billeted at Brookhill, and the French Government awarded Kingswear the Médaille d'Argent. Source: [Liberation Route, Free French naval memorial at Kingswear](https://www.europeremembers.com/pois/2604/free-french-naval-memorial-at-kingswear); [Coastal Forces Heritage forum](https://cfv.org.uk/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4495)
- **Verified (1975).** Darthaven Marina Limited was incorporated on 11 September 1975 (Companies House data), at Brixham Road, Kingswear. The marina did not exist in 1967. Source: [CorpSignals, Darthaven Marina Ltd 01226089](https://corpsignals.uk/companies/uk/darthaven-marina-ltd-01226089.html); [Companies House](https://pay.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/01226089/officers)
- **Verified (after 1972).** The Dart Valley Railway recouped its purchase cost mostly from surplus land: at Goodrington (later flats) and at Kingswear, "which became a marina". This links the marina site to former railway land. Source: [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Steam Railway (search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway)
- **Unverified.** One walking guide says that "until fairly recently a fish quay occupied the area where the boatyard and its associated marina are now". It does not say which marina or which years. Source: [search extract (Explore Devon / Discover Dartmouth walk guides)](https://discoverdartmouth.com/discover-kingswear)

**Kingswear Castle**
- **Verified (1491-1502).** Built 1491-1502 as a coastal artillery tower, designed to work with Dartmouth Castle across the river. Source: [Historic England via British Listed Buildings 101108543](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101108543-kingswear-castle-kingswear)
- **Verified (1855 to 1987).** Restored in 1855 by Charles Seale Hayne as a summer house (architect Thomas Lidstone). It stayed a private residence, apart from a wartime occupation by Royal Marines. The Landmark Trust bought it in 1987 and finished restoring it in 1990. Source: [Landmark Trust history album (PDF)](https://cms.landmarktrust.org.uk/globalassets/3.-images-and-documents-to-keep/history-albums/kingswear-castle-history-album.pdf); [Wikipedia, Kingswear Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_Castle)
- **Unverified (1955).** Sir Frederic Bennett MP bought the castle in 1955 and made it his private residence and constituency headquarters (single secondary timeline). Wikipedia says Bennett retired from politics in 1987 before selling. If correct, in 1967 the castle was the Conservative MP's private house, not open to the public. Source: [search extract, Kingswear Castle timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_Castle)

**Church of St Thomas of Canterbury**

Hayward of Exeter, in local limestone rubble with freestone dressings, Decorated style. A Grade II lychgate south of the church dates from c.1847, probably also by Hayward. Source: [Historic England 1108544](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1108544); [British Listed Buildings, lychgate](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101324861-lychgate-south-of-church-of-st-thomas-of-canterbury-kingswear)
- **Conflict (tower date).** The listing dates the tower to the 14th century; Wikipedia gives c.1173. Source: [Historic England 1108544](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1108544); [Wikipedia, Church of St Thomas of Canterbury, Kingswear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St_Thomas_of_Canterbury,_Kingswear)

### Inferences
- In June 1967 the goods yard and quayside sidings would have stood idle: freight stopped in 1964-65 and the tracks were lifted only later. Grass-grown, rusty sidings by the river, perhaps with stored wagons or boats, would be credible. The tracks still being in place is an inference, not verified.
- The waterfront north of the station (the later Darthaven and Hoodown) was railway and ex-wartime land in 1967, with no marina pontoons. Moorings would have been swinging buoys and trots in the river, not finger pontoons. This follows from the 1975 marina company and the mid-1980s Hoodown depot.
- The 1960 lever frame and the box's survival to October 1968 mean semaphore signalling and a manned box should appear in a 1967 scene.
- Kingswear Castle in 1967 should look like a lived-in private house: domestic windows, curtains, perhaps a car. It should not look like a holiday let or a ruin.

Leads: SRS diagram S918 (1961), Great Western Railway Journal No. 25 (1998), NLS 1:2500 OS sheets of the 1950s-60s.

## 2. The Paignton–Kingswear railway in 1967

### Takeaway
In June 1967 the line was British Railways Western Region. Since 18 April 1966 most trains had been diesel multiple-unit shuttles from Paignton, with through trains only at peak season. Summer 1967 was the last season of regular Sunday trains (withdrawn from 24 September 1967). Steam had gone by about 1963-64 (not firmly dated). Goods had ended in 1964-65. The line was sold on 30 December 1972 to the Dart Valley Railway, which began running on 1 January 1973.

### Cited Findings

**Operator and service pattern**
- **Verified (1966).** From 18 April 1966 most services ran as a shuttle from Paignton, with peak-season trains the exception. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station (search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)
- **Verified (1967).** Sunday trains were withdrawn from 24 September 1967, though some ran in summer 1968. Sunday trains no longer called at Churston after the 1967 summer season. So June 1967 still had Sunday trains. Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station); [Wikipedia, Churston railway station](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churston_railway_station)
- **Verified (1962 baseline).** On summer Saturdays in 1962, 7 trains ran Paddington–Kingswear, plus 4 terminating at Paignton. Source: [Cornwall Railway Society, Newton Abbot to Kingswear](https://www.cornwallrailwaysociety.org.uk/newton-abbot-to-kingswear-also-brixham-branch.html)
- **Verified (1967, but destination uncertain).** The "Torbay Express" name was dropped from the daily service in 1967 and applied to a Saturday-only train from Paddington at 10.50. The extract does not say whether that train ran beyond Paignton to Kingswear in 1967. Source: [Wikipedia, Torbay Express (search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torbay_Express)

The thread concerns Paddington–Ilfracombe/Minehead workings, so it may not apply to Kingswear. **Run-down, closure and sale**

Dart Valley services began on Monday 1 January 1973. Source: [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Steam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway); [railcar.co.uk](https://www.railcar.co.uk/operations/western-region/paignton-kingswear); [Kingsbridge Today](https://kingsbridge-today.co.uk/news/dart-valley-rail-celebrates-50-years-of-operation-599118)

### Inferences
- A credible June 1967 scene at Kingswear shows a green BR DMU (two- or three-car, Class 116 or similar) on the Paignton shuttle. On a summer Saturday, a Warship-hauled through train from Paddington is plausible but not confirmed for 1967; a Warship in green or maroon is safest, and blue is possible. Steam locomotives would be wrong for regular 1967 service.
- No goods trains: there should be no coal wagons being shunted at Kingswear in 1967, though some stored wagons on the idle sidings would not be implausible (unverified).
- The station was staffed (booking office, porters at both pontoons) and the box was manned. The canopy and shed still had BR WR signage; colours are not verified.

- A documented date for the last regular steam working to Kingswear. Not found.
- DMU livery in June 1967. I found no source for this branch. Background knowledge (unverified): BR multiple-unit green with a small yellow warning panel was standard until about 1967, when full yellow ends and then blue/blue-grey began to appear. Needs a dated colour photograph to confirm.

## 3. The harbour mouth: castles, church, daymark, lights, buoys and coves

### Takeaway
The mouth in 1967 was defined by Dartmouth Castle and St Petrox church on the west bank and Kingswear Castle (a private house) on the east, with the 1864 daymark on the skyline above Froward Point. The most important 1967-specific detail is the Kingswear light: in 1967 it was the original 1864 white-painted stone tower, about 11 m (36 ft), beside Kingswear Castle. It was demolished as unsafe in 1980 and replaced in 1981 by today's small squat white GRP tower. Brownstone Battery's WWII buildings, decommissioned in 1956, stood empty on the cliffs east of the mouth.

### Cited Findings

**Kingswear lighthouse (leading light)**
- **Verified (1864 to 1980).** The station was established in 1864. The original light was an 11 m hexagonal stone tower on the cliff, demolished in 1980 because it had become unsafe. The current light dates from 1981. It is Dartmouth's entrance leading light, showing Iso WRG 3s, visible 9 nautical miles (modern characteristic). Source: [Lighthouse Digest database 1917](https://www.lighthousedepot.com/database/uniquelighthouse.cfm?value=1917); [Lighthouses of the UK: SW England (ibiblio)](https://ibiblio.org/lighthouse/engsw.htm)
- **Conflict (shape).** A Devon guide gives "a white painted hexagonal stone lighthouse 36 feet high as part of Kingswear Castle", replaced by 1981 by a "small squat white painted circular GRP tower". Another source calls the 1864 tower octagonal, 36 ft, attached to the cliff. Source: [mycetes.co.uk Devon lights page](https://www.mycetes.co.uk/a/page65.html); [ibiblio](https://ibiblio.org/lighthouse/engsw.htm)
- **Conflict (who operates it).** Dart Harbour and Navigation Authority (owner, per one listing) versus Trinity House (managing body, per Lighthouse Digest). Source: [Lighthouse Digest](https://www.lighthousedepot.com/database/uniquelighthouse.cfm?value=1917)

**Kingswear Daymark (Day Beacon)**
- **Verified (1864).** An open-based octagonal tower about 24 m (80 ft) high, built in 1864 by the Dart Harbour Commissioners on the headland at Froward Point. It marks the hard-to-see harbour entrance. Seale Hayne leased the land the year after he helped found the Harbour Commission (1863). Grade II listed. Its elevation is about 145-150 m above sea level. Source: [Historic England 1108546](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1108546); [British Listed Buildings](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101108546-the-tower-kingswear); [Wikipedia, Kingswear Daymark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_Daymark)
- **Conflict (material and height).** The listing says slate rubble; another source says limestone. An information board says 25 m against 24 m or 80 ft elsewhere. Source: [Historic England 1108546](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1108546); [greyarro.ws](https://greyarro.ws/t/the-daymark-kingswear-added-to-lighthouses-in-south-west/275)

**Dartmouth Castle, the chain, Godmerock**
- **Verified (late 15th century).** Edward IV offered the burgesses £30 a year "for ever" to build "a tower with a chain sufficient in length and strength to stretch across the mouth of the haven". The chain ran to the Kingswear bank, held up by small boats ("cobbellys"), and was protected there by a tower at Godmerock. The castle is described as the first built for gunpowder artillery. It was not permanently garrisoned. Source: [Britain Express, Dartmouth Castle](https://britainexpress.com/counties/devon/castles/dartmouth.htm); [English Heritage, history of Dartmouth Castle](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/dartmouth-castle/history/)
- **Conflict (completion date).** One source credits Henry VII with expanding the castle; another gives the tower's completion as 1495. Source: [Britain Express](https://britainexpress.com/counties/devon/castles/dartmouth.htm)
- **Verified (today).** The site is managed by English Heritage. Source: [English Heritage](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/dartmouth-castle/history/)

**St Petrox church**
- **Conflict (origin).** St Petrox stands within the castle walls. A walk guide claims a monk's cell of 894; the Devon HER says a chapel existed by the late 12th century. It was enlarged or rebuilt in 1641 and became a parish in 1838. Source: [South West Coast Path, Dartmouth Castle walk](https://www.southwestcoastpath.org.uk/walksdb/407/printable/); [British Listed Buildings, Church of St Petrox](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101297086-church-of-st-petrox-dartmouth/photos)
- **Verified (detail).** The remains of the archway of the gate from the harbour form part of the churchyard wall, visible from the Castle Ferry steps. Source: [Slow Europe forum (visitor description)](https://sloweurope.com/community/threads/dartmouth-castle-and-st-petrox-church.6440/)

**Entrance buoys (modern; 1967 not confirmed)**
- **Verified (present day).** Castle Ledge is marked by a green conical buoy, which also marks the harbour authority's limit (6 kn limit inside). Checkstone is a red can buoy about 35 m east of Kitten Rock. Homestone is a red can on the outermost shallow patch. The Western Blackstone rocks are unlit. Compulsory pilotage starts at the Inner Froward Point–Combe Point line. Source: [Visit My Harbour, Dartmouth](https://www.visitmyharbour.com/harbour/dartmouth-marinas/); [Royal Dart YC, Avoiding Rocks (2023)](https://royaldart.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Avoiding_Rocks.pdf); [Dart Harbour, pilotage](https://www.dartharbour.org/commercial-2/pilotage/)

**Brownstone Battery and Froward Point (east of the mouth)**
- **Conflict (date and guns).** A WWII coastal battery: most sources give 1940 (National Trust), one 1942. Most say two ex-navy 6-inch guns; one says two 9.2-inch. It was manned by up to 300 soldiers and had gun emplacements, searchlight positions down steps to the shore, and an observation post. It was decommissioned in 1956 (single listing). Source: [National Trust, history of Brownstone Battery](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/devon/dartmouth-and-kingswear-countryside/the-history-of-brownstone-battery); [South West Coast Path, Kingswear and Brownstone Battery](https://www.southwestcoastpath.org.uk/walksdb/93/); [Historic England photo, western searchlight](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/photos/item/890524)
- **Verified (today).** The observation post is now used by the National Coastwatch Institution (NCI Froward Point), a later use. Source: [National Trust](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/devon/dartmouth-and-kingswear-countryside/the-history-of-brownstone-battery); [Wikipedia, NCI Froward Point](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/NCI_Froward_Point)

**Castle Ferry, Warfleet, Castle Cove**
- **Verified (today).** The Castle Ferry is a small open boat running between Dartmouth town and steep, sometimes slippery steps at the castle. Sources disagree on the town departure point (Dartmouth Quay or Stumpy Steps). I found no 1960s information. Source: [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/england/southwest-england/dartmouth/attractions/dartmouth-castle/a/poi-sig/1019764/359010); [Slow Europe forum](https://sloweurope.com/community/threads/dartmouth-castle-and-st-petrox-church.6440/)
- **Verified (photo reference).** A GWR scenic print "Dartmouth, Kingswear, Warfleet c1900" exists (the Steam Museum of the GWR). It is too early for 1967 but shows the coastline. Source: [GWR scenic print](https://prints.aap.com.au/steam-museum-of-the-gwr/gwr-scenic-views/devon/dartmouth-kingswear-warfleet-c1900-8341348.html)

### Inferences
- For 1967, model the Kingswear light as a taller (about 11 m, 36 ft) white-painted multi-sided stone tower attached to the cliff by Kingswear Castle, not the present squat GRP drum. This is one of the clearest lost features.
- Brownstone Battery in 1967 would be about 11 years after decommissioning: empty concrete buildings, guns removed. That the guns were gone is an inference; no source states it. There was no NCI lookout yet.
- Dartmouth Castle in 1967 was presumably in state guardianship (Ministry of Public Building and Works). English Heritage was formed only in 1984. Background knowledge, not verified in this session.
- Godmerock, the chain-tower site on the Kingswear bank, should appear as a ruin or fragment near Kingswear Castle. Its 1967 state was not found.

### Gaps
- The 1967 buoyage and lights at the entrance: which buoys existed, their colours and shapes (pre-IALA, before 1977-80), and whether Castle Ledge was then a conical green or a black buoy. Before the IALA System A changeover, starboard-hand buoys in UK waters were typically black; that is background knowledge and unverified here. Lead: Admiralty chart 2253 (Dartmouth Harbour), a 1960s edition.
- Whether Dartmouth Castle or Bayard's Cove carried navigation lights in 1967. No source found.
- The Castle Ferry's operator, boat and route in 1967. No source found.
- Warfleet Creek and Castle Cove in 1967: no specific findings. Warfleet's buildings (former brewery, limekilns) were not checked.
- The state of Godmerock in 1967.

## 4. The east bank at Noss and the Higher Ferry, seen from Kingswear (brief)

### Takeaway
North of Kingswear the east bank held the railway line to the Higher Ferry level crossing (Britannia Halt) and the Philip & Son shipyard at Noss. Shipyards are covered by another researcher.

### Cited Findings
- **Verified (1928 photo).** "The Philip & Son Ltd Shipyard, Higher Noss Point, 1928" (Britain from Above EPW024184), and a 1928 view of ships at moorings at Maypool (EPW023971). Source: [Britain from Above EPW024184](https://britainfromabove.org.uk/en/image/EPW024184); [Britain from Above EPW023971](https://britainfromabove.org.uk/en/image/EPW023971)
- **Verified (WWII remains).** A D-Day landing-craft maintenance site lies immediately south of Lower Noss Point, Kingswear (Historic England Heritage at Risk). Source: [Historic England Heritage at Risk 36119](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/heritage-at-risk/search-register/list-entry/36119)
- **Verified (1877 to now).** Britannia Halt (originally Kingswear Crossing Halt, 1877) stood at the Higher Ferry level crossing; it has since been demolished. In 1991 the whole line's signalling was concentrated at Britannia Crossing. Source: [Dartmouth Steam Railway (Wikishire mirror)](https://wikishire.co.uk/wiki/Paignton_and_Dartmouth_Steam_Railway)
- **Verified (existing project notes).** The Higher Ferry floating bridge runs Sandquay–Noss; vessel No. 7 was built in 1960. Source: SOURCES.md, citing [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Higher Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Higher_Ferry)

### Inferences
- From Kingswear in 1967, the Noss works would read as an active industrial shipyard (sheds, slipways, cranes), not the present Noss on Dart marina.

### Gaps
- The appearance of the Noss works and the size of its workforce in 1967 are left to the shipyards researcher.

## 5. What has changed: features of 1967 now gone, and features of today absent in 1967

### Takeaway
The biggest differences are on the railway waterfront: in 1967 BR goods yard and quayside sidings, a working BR signal box and the old 1864 lighthouse tower; today Darthaven Marina (1975 onwards), the Hoodown depot (mid-1980s), a GRP light (1981) and heritage steam branding (1973 onwards).

### Cited Findings
**Present in 1967, gone or changed since**
- **Verified (gone 1980).** The original 1864 Kingswear lighthouse tower was demolished in 1980. Source: [Lighthouse Digest](https://www.lighthousedepot.com/database/uniquelighthouse.cfm?value=1917)
- **Verified (layout changed).** The goods yard is now a car park and boat storage, and the quayside tracks were later removed (removal date unverified). Source: [Wikipedia, Kingswear railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station); [RMweb](https://www.rmweb.co.uk/forums/topic/179823-kingswear-track-layout/)
- **Verified (BR era ended 1972).** The BR-operated railway and passenger ferry ended in 1972. Source: [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Steam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Steam_Railway); [Wikipedia, Dartmouth Passenger Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Passenger_Ferry)
- **Verified (gone).** Britannia Halt (demolished, date unknown) and the Churston crossing loop (closed 20 October 1968; a loop exists at Churston today, reinstated by the heritage railway). That the loop was reinstated is unverified in this session. Source: [Wikipedia, Churston railway station](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churston_railway_station)
- **Verified (status changed).** The Royal Dart Hotel became apartments. Source: [Hydrock](https://www.hydrock.com/projects/royal-dart-dartmouth)
- **Verified (status changed 1987).** Kingswear Castle went from private residence to Landmark Trust holiday let. Source: [Landmark Trust](https://cms.landmarktrust.org.uk/globalassets/3.-images-and-documents-to-keep/history-albums/kingswear-castle-history-album.pdf)

**Present today, absent in 1967**
- **Verified (1975).** Darthaven Marina and its pontoons and boatyard (company formed 1975). Source: [CorpSignals](https://corpsignals.uk/companies/uk/darthaven-marina-ltd-01226089.html)
- **Verified (mid-1980s).** The DHNA Hoodown facility. Source: [Dart Harbour](https://dartharbour.org/harbour-information/sailing-directions)
- **Verified (1981).** The GRP Kingswear light. Source: [Lighthouse Digest](https://www.lighthousedepot.com/database/uniquelighthouse.cfm?value=1917)
- **Verified (1991).** The Britannia Crossing signalling panel. Source: [Wikishire, Dartmouth Steam Railway](https://wikishire.co.uk/wiki/Paignton_and_Dartmouth_Steam_Railway)
- **Conflict (opening date; either way after 1967).** Greenway Halt (1990s or 2012). Source: [Wikipedia, Greenway Halt](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenway_Halt_railway_station_(Devon))
- **Verified (after 1956).** The National Coastwatch lookout at Froward Point. Source: [Wikipedia, NCI Froward Point](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/NCI_Froward_Point)
- **Verified (1974, 1991).** The refurbished and redeveloped Royal Dart Yacht Club clubhouse. Source: [Wikipedia, Royal Dart Yacht Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dart_Yacht_Club)

### Inferences
