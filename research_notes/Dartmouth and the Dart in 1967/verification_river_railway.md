# Verification pass: river, ferries, vessels, shipyard, railway and harbour mouth (June 1967)

**Status of this file: final save of this pass.** Scope: the report sections "Kingswear was a British Railways terminus with a dead goods yard" and "The river had new ferry tugs, an all-motor trip fleet and two dead paddle steamers", plus the related conflict and gap rows. Baseline June 1967.

## The three things that change the report most

1. **There were three idle paddle steamers on the Dart in June 1967, not two.** Besides *Kingswear Castle* (Old Mill Creek) and *Totnes Castle*, the former Weymouth steamer *Consul*, renamed *Duke of Devonshire*, lay "on the Kingswear side just below the Higher Ferry" as a sailing-school ship from February 1965 until she was towed away in October 1968. *Totnes Castle* was not a bare hulk: from 1964 she carried large flat-roofed sheds on deck as sailing-school accommodation.
2. **The branch train's livery is now sourced: unlined green with a small yellow warning panel.** A dated caption gives "unlined green" for the 15.58 Paignton to Kingswear on 4 October 1966. Rail blue was only just appearing in Devon in June 1967.
3. **The river boats were white, not dark blue, and the Higher Ferry was owned by the shipyard.** The River Dart Steamboat Co.'s motor vessels had white hulls and cabin sides with varnished wheelhouses and no funnels. Philip & Son Ltd owned the Higher Ferry from 1918 to at least 1999.

The old Kingswear lighthouse could **not** be verified: no allowed host carries a page about it.

- Pages were fetched with curl and read as text. ## New findings

### 1. A third paddle steamer: *Duke of Devonshire* (ex-*Consul*), 1965–68

- Arrived under her own steam on 4 February 1965: "Consul then proceeded down river to moor head out on the buoys off Kingswear" ([kingswearcastle.org, "4th February 1965: Consul"](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/musings/4th-february-1965-consul/), 2021).
- **Position in 1967:** "In due course Consul was moved a little further up river to moorings on the Kingswear side just below the Higher Ferry where she was fitted out for her new role as a sailing school." (same page)
- **Dates:** "Consul as Duke of Devonshire had only three seasons in this new capacity before the business failed. She was towed to the scrapyard in Southampton in October 1968." June 1967 falls in her third season, so she was there and in use.
- **Name on the hull in 1967:** *Duke of Devonshire* ("now back with her original name"). "Built in 1896" (site search extract for the 10 December 1937 article).
- **Appearance (picture, monochrome, "as a sailing school on the River Dart"):** a larger sea-going paddle steamer than the Dart boats, lying against the wooded Kingswear bank; dark hull with a pale upper strake and pale paddle box; a tall pale funnel with a dark top; a raised bridge over a deckhouse with a stair up each side; one tall mast aft of the bridge; boats in davits.
- Condition: "her decks were prone to leak"; "the accommodation was very basic".
- The harbour authority "did require her to be able to shift ship under her own power".

### 2. *Totnes Castle* in the mid-1960s

### 4. Higher Ferry No. 7 (1960): appearance

- **Layout** (pictures on [Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html)): a flat steel pontoon hull with a hinged loading prow at each end, each prow hung on chains from a tall steel gallows frame; one paddle wheel each side amidships under a plain curved casing; on one side a deckhouse with a tall, narrow two-storey control tower and a flagstaff with a Red Ensign; on the other side a low deckhouse carrying advertisement boards (one reads "Relyon Mattresses and Divans"); lattice gates across each end; a red-and-white lifebuoy on each gallows leg; a small boat in davits.
- **Mid-1960s colours** (John Hinde card, photo E. Ludwig; Simplon dates it by "Austin A40, Hillman Imp and Rover 90", so 1963 or later): hull, gallows and superstructure **very pale grey, almost white; red-oxide boot-topping**; paddle casing mid-grey; white gates.
- **About 1967–68 colours** (N.P.O. Dexter card): superstructure **white above, mid-green below**; gallows white or pale grey; gates and rails orange-red. Cars: a Ford Corsair registered EMD 817B (1964) and a Ford Cortina Mk2 whose plate I read as "TYL 874F". An F suffix means August 1967 or later, but the reading comes from an enlarged scan and is not certain.
- **For June 1967:** pale grey with red boot-topping is the better-supported choice; green and white cannot be ruled out.
- **Size:** "The new ferry is approximately 10 metres longer and 6 metres wider than the old vessel." ([Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html), quoting the operator). Against the game's own figure of 52 m × 18 m for the 2009 ferry, that makes No. 7 **about 42 m × 12 m**. The subtraction is my inference.
- **Crossing:** about 1,100 ft (335 m): "This Bridge could cross the 1100 feet waterway in 3 minutes" (of the 1876 ferry; [DHRG](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/harbour-papers-part-iv-the-ferries/)).
- **Sound:** "the familiar flap, flap, flap of the floats beating the water" ([kingswearcastle.org](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/?p=17274)).

This suggests Kingswear already had some coloured render in the 1960s, against the report's "grey limestone" picture. Both are retouched or small pictures: treat as a lead.

### 9. Railway

- Class 116 units in 1967 were non-gangwayed suburban sets: "the fitting of gangways to all the remaining WR sets was well under way" only about 1970 ([railcar.co.uk](https://www.railcar.co.uk/type/class-116/operations)).
- Britannia Crossing in a colour thumbnail of 25 September 1969 ([railcar.co.uk](https://www.railcar.co.uk/images/18273)): a short platform with a small canopy with a sawtooth valance; **white wooden level-crossing gates with a red disc**; a white crossing-keeper's house with a slate roof; a swan-neck lamp.
- Kingswear in 1967 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)): platform "extended in 1929 to 850 feet (259 m)"; "a long footbridge constructed over the station to carry a footpath to the shore of the river" (1895); a double-track steel Hoodown viaduct of 1928 just outside the station.

### 10. Other context

- The 1967 season was poor partly because of "the sinking of the Torrey Canyon" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Dart_Steamboat_Co)).
- Two sailing schools used old paddle steamers as bases between 1964 and 1968, so school sailing dinghies are a sourced presence on the river.
- The naval college's ship name was HMS *Dartmouth*: "The college was renamed HMS Dartmouth in 1953" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Royal_Naval_College)).
