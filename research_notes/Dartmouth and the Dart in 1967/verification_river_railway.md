Scope: the report sections "Kingswear was a British Railways terminus with a dead goods yard" and "The river had new ferry tugs, an all-motor trip fleet and two dead paddle steamers", plus the related conflict and gap rows. Baseline June 1967.

## How to read this file

- **Confirmed** = I opened the page and the full text says it. **Corrected** = the full text says something different, or the cited page does not carry the claim. **Still unverified** = no page I could open states it.
- "Read in full" pages were fetched with curl and read as text. Postcards and photographs on Simplon and railcar.co.uk were downloaded and looked at; colour descriptions from them are **my own reading of a scanned postcard** (John Hinde cards were colour-retouched at the time), so treat hues as approximate.
- Year of each source is given in the evidence column.

## New findings (interim)

### Higher Ferry No. 7 (1960): appearance

- **Layout** (from four 1960s–1990s postcards and photographs on [Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html)): a flat steel pontoon hull with a hinged loading prow at each end, each prow raised by chains from a tall steel gallows frame; one paddle wheel each side amidships under a plain curved steel casing; on one side, a deckhouse with a tall, narrow two-storey control tower (wheelhouse on top) carrying a flagstaff with a Red Ensign; a small deckhouse with advertisement boards on the other side; lattice gates across each end of the car deck; a small boat in davits; a red-and-white lifebuoy on each gallows leg.
- **Colours, early to mid 1960s** (John Hinde card, photo E. Ludwig; Simplon dates it by "Austin A40, Hillman Imp and Rover 90", so 1963 or later): hull, gallows and superstructure **very pale grey, almost white**; **red-oxide boot-topping**; paddle casing mid-grey; white gates.
- **Colours, 1967–68 onward** (N.P.O. Dexter card): superstructure **white above, mid-green below**, gallows frames white or pale grey, gates and rails orange-red, the raised prow buff-yellow underneath. The cars are a Ford Corsair registered EMD 817B (1964) and a Ford Cortina Mk2 whose plate I read as "TYL 874F" (an F suffix would mean August 1967 or later; the reading is from an enlarged scan and is not certain).
- **So for June 1967**: Simplon's sequence is "grey, green, red and then blue". The change from grey to green fell between about 1963 and 1968. Pale grey with red boot-topping is the safer choice for early June 1967; white-and-green cannot be ruled out.
- **Size**: "The new ferry is approximately 10 metres longer and 6 metres wider than the old vessel" ([Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html), quoting the operator, 2009). The game's own figure for the 2009 ferry is 52 m × 18 m, which would make No. 7 **about 42 m × 12 m**. That subtraction is my inference, not a sourced dimension.
- **Crossing**: "This Bridge could cross the 1100 feet waterway in 3 minutes" (of the 1876 ferry; [DHRG ferries paper](https://dartmouth-history.org.uk/dhrg-archive/harbour-papers-part-iv-the-ferries/)). About 335 m.
- **Sound**: "the familiar flap, flap, flap of the floats beating the water" ([kingswearcastle.org, 2007](https://www.kingswearcastle.org/?p=17274)).

### Lower Ferry in the 1960s: appearance

- 1960s John Hinde card (photo E. Ludwig; cars "Austin A40 and Ford Anglia"), [Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/DartCarFerries.html): the **float** is a low timber-decked pontoon with a hinged timber ramp at each end hung on chains from four white-painted posts, white tubular side rails and white lattice gates, a row of old tyres along the side as fenders, cars two abreast (about six to eight aboard). The **tug** lashed alongside has a **black hull, a red boot-topping, a buff strake at the gunwale, and a small red-brown (varnished or red-painted) wheelhouse**, with tyres hung along the side. This card may predate the 1965–66 steel tugs.
- A later "Dixon" card on the same page shows a tug with a **buff-yellow wheelhouse and black hull** beside a float with white rails; this is probably one of the 1965–66 Philip-built tugs, but the card is undated.
- The floats were **numbered** ("Float II", "Float III"); no names in the 1960s.

### British Railways passenger ferry (Adrian Gilbert, Humphrey Gilbert)

- **Dimensions** (Humphrey Gilbert): length overall 57.9 ft (17.6 m), waterline 56 ft, beam 13.4 ft (4.1 m), draught 4.9 ft, 34.65 tons; single screw; present-day certificate 128 passengers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Humphrey_Gilbert)). DHRG: "55′ wooden motor boats … 35 ton".
- **Shape** (two black-and-white photographs "in BR service", [Simplon](https://simplonpc.co.uk/BR_Devon.html)): a long, low launch with a **dark hull** and a white line at the sheer; an open well deck forward where passengers stand; a raised wheelhouse about one-third back with lifebuoys on its front; a low saloon aft with a row of square windows and a clerestory roof; a short funnel or exhaust casing abaft the wheelhouse; ensign at the stern. Superstructure reads as mid-toned wood (varnished) in one view. Hull colour cannot be told from monochrome: black or dark blue.
- Named "after two famous local explorers from Greenway House" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Humphrey_Gilbert)).

### River Dart Steamboat Co. motor vessels

- **Colour photographs of the period** on [Simplon](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html): Seymour Castle (Dennis card T.0601, at Totnes) has a **white hull, a black rubbing band at deck level, a dark boot-topping, a varnished teak-coloured wheelhouse, a white canvas awning on a white pipe frame, white rails, and a red swallow-tailed house flag** at the masthead. Berry Castle (N.P.O. Dexter card B7113) is the same: white hull, black band, **red boot-topping**, varnished wheelhouse. An earlier Plastichrome card shows Berry Castle with a **black hull and a white bulwark strake** carrying the name; so she changed from black to white at some date before the later card.
- **Conway Castle** (company postcards and launch photograph, black and white): pale (white) hull with a dark boot-topping; long open promenade deck with awning frame; a varnished wooden wheelhouse set on a flying bridge amidships; rails all round; name on the bow in dark letters.
- **House flag**: the company's one-shilling souvenir brochure shows a **red pennant with white letters "R·D·S"** ([Simplon](https://www.simplonpc.co.uk/RiverDartSBCo.html)).
- **Dartmouth Castle as built** (her 1967 form): "open decks forward and aft of a lower deck saloon, and an open promenade deck above, with the wheelhouse at its forward end. This wheelhouse was … originally fitted to the previous Dartmouth Castle, a 1907 vintage paddle steamer. The promenade deck also featured a number of very tall ventilators, a canopy above, and a rather ugly landing deck" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Dartmouth_Castle)). No funnel until the late-1970s rebuild.
- **Kingswear Castle dimensions**: 113.75 ft (34.67 m) overall, 108.14 ft between perpendiculars, beam 17.43 ft (5.31 m; this is the hull, not across the paddle boxes), draught 3.43 ft ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_Kingswear_Castle)).

### Railway

- The 25 September 1969 colour thumbnail of Britannia Crossing Halt ([railcar.co.uk](https://www.railcar.co.uk/images/18273)) shows a short platform with a small canopy with a sawtooth valance, **white-painted wooden level-crossing gates with a red disc**, a white crossing-keeper's house with a slate roof, and a swan-neck lamp. All of this predates 1967.
- Kingswear station details that stood in 1967 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswear_railway_station)): platform "extended in 1929 to 850 feet (259 m)"; "a long footbridge constructed over the station to carry a footpath to the shore of the river" (1895); double-track steel Hoodown viaduct of 1928 just outside the station; a turntable.
