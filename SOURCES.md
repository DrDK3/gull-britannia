# Source notes: Dartmouth, 1967

*Gull Britannia* is set in an alternate 1967 in which Soviet forces occupy southern England. The occupation, its people and its paperwork are fiction. The town underneath it should be recognisable, so this file records what was checked, where it came from, and what is approximated or invented.

Research was done on 11 November 2026 with web search. Items marked **verified** were confirmed against at least one of the sources listed at the bottom. Items marked **unverified** are general knowledge that I did not re-check, and should be confirmed before they are relied on.

## Verified

| Detail | How it appears in the game | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Boat Float is an enclosed, roughly trapezoidal tidal mooring. It has reclaimed land on its north, west and south sides; the South Embankment (c.1885–1889; sources differ) closes the east side. A cast-iron bridge carries the road over the entrance. | Rectangular basin opening under an Embankment bridge with green-painted girders. A gull can fly under it. | British Listed Buildings 101218121; South West Coast Path *Dartmouth Town Trail* |

Requisitioned as the officers' mess. It is the Bomber Wing's roost in Mission 2. | South West Coast Path trail; Discover Dartmouth |
| The Butterwalk dates from 1635–40. It has granite piers (13 built, 11 survive), jettied timber-framed upper floors, and is Grade I listed. It was damaged by a bomb in 1943, restored in the 1950s, and houses the museum. | An arcaded row on the north side of Duke Street with 11 piers and jettied black-and-white upper floors. The player starts on its roof in Mission 2. | Historic England list entry 1197518; Dartmoor Trust archive |

**Conflicts noted:**
- The Embankment is dated 1885 by the walking trails and c.1889 by the listing.
- One listing quote says Bayard's Cove quay only reached the fort in 1839; English Heritage dates the quay to the early 16th century.

The game depicts the quay as it stood by 1967, so neither date matters to the build.

## Unverified: check before relying on these

- Pre-decimal currency, still in use in 1967 (decimalisation came in February 1971). Not yet used on screen.
- The BBC Home Service became Radio 4 on 30 September 1967. Not yet used; intended for a later briefing.
- Red K6 telephone kiosks and GPO pillar boxes. Both are modelled.
- The Morris Minor and Ford Anglia as everyday cars, and the GAZ-69 and ZIL-157 as period Soviet vehicles. All are modelled as simple primitives.
- Britannia Royal Naval College on the hill above the town. Modelled as a red-brick block with a white tower, standing in for the fictional *Kommandatura*.
- St Saviour's Church near the Boat Float, with a battlemented west tower. Its massing is simplified.
- St Thomas' church tower and Kingswear Castle on the Kingswear side, and Dartmouth Castle at the harbour mouth. All are simplified backdrop.
- Language for European institutions in 1967. People said "the Common Market" (the EEC), and Britain was outside it (de Gaulle vetoed its second application in November 1967). NATO's headquarters moved to Belgium in 1967. None of this is used in-game yet; it is reserved for later briefings.

## Deliberate approximations

- **Scale and layout.** The waterfront is compressed, roughly 1:2 to 1:3, and the river is narrower. The streets behind the waterfront are simplified into blocks, and the hillsides are smooth slopes lined with terraces. Kingswear is a backdrop.
- **Shop names are invented and not historical:** *W. Pengelly · Baker · Pasties*, *R. Nancekivell · Family Butcher*, *W. H. Gillard · Ironmonger*, *Penrose · Wines & Spirits*, *Harbour Teas*, *Quay Stores*, *Ship Chandler*. The *Post Office* and *Fish & Chips* are generic.
- **The Royal Avenue Gardens bandstand** is a placeholder; its form and position are unverified.
- **The station's clock turret, canopy and pontoon** are approximate shapes.
- **Bayard's Cove Fort** is modelled as a simple ring with gun ports; the real plan is not reproduced.
- **Boats:** *Dorothy May*, *Brixham Lass*, the patrol boat *ПК-117* and the submarine *S-197* are all fictional.
- **The occupation is fiction throughout:** the Kommandatura, curfew notices, the checkpoint and the uniforms (simplified Soviet-style caps and greatcoats, with no insignia beyond a red star).
- **Cyrillic text** should be checked by a Russian speaker:
  - "КОМЕНДАТУРА" (Kommandatura)
  - "КОМЕНДАТУРА ПОРТА" (Port Kommandatura)
  - "КОРМИТЬ ЧАЕК ЗАПРЕЩЕНО" (Feeding gulls prohibited)
  - "СЛАВА СОВЕТСКОЙ АРМИИ!" (Glory to the Soviet Army!)
- **Characters are all original:** Operative SCRUMPY, Mother, Pte. Borovsky, Lt Voronova, Col. Grushkov, the Bomber Wing (Gristle, Mrs Wimbush, Old Nelson, The Padre).

## Assets and attribution

No third-party assets are redistributed.

- **Geometry, textures and audio:** every mesh is built from Godot primitives or generated geometry. Surface patterns (slate, cobbles, stone, water, flags) are original procedural shaders. All sound effects and music are synthesised at runtime by `scripts/core/sfx.gd` from original note data.
- **Fonts:** Godot's built-in default font, plus an optional lookup of *system* fonts (typewriter and serif families) through `SystemFont`. No font files ship with the project.
- **Reference photographs** informed nothing beyond the facts above, and none are included.
