# Source notes: Dartmouth, 1967

*Gull Britannia* is set in an alternate 1967 in which Soviet forces occupy southern England. The occupation, its people and its paperwork are fiction. The town underneath it is real: since version 2 the game is built on [SailSim](https://drdk3.github.io/SailSim/)'s River Dart, made from open survey data (terrain, buildings, roads, quays, the river), and taken back to 1967. This file records what was checked, where it came from, what was changed for 1967, and what is approximated or invented.

Historical research was done on 11 November 2026 with web search. Items marked **verified** were confirmed against at least one of the sources listed at the bottom. Items marked **unverified** are general knowledge that was not re-checked and should be confirmed before they are relied on.

## The map: SailSim's River Dart

The terrain, the town's buildings (footprints, heights, roofs), roads, quay walls, the river's bed and its water area are SailSim's, generated by SailSim's open-data pipeline (its `docs/GEODATA.md`), at their real positions. Game positions are SailSim's local metres east and north of the Royal Dart Yacht Club (50.34801 N, 3.57339 W); heights are metres above Ordnance Datum Newlyn.

The gull collides with what is drawn. When the game loads, it renders the scenery from straight above into an off-screen height map (0.5 m cells over the old town, 2 m over the harbour) and reads it back, so roofs, chimneys, quays, walls and trees are solid where the picture shows them (`src/gull/view/heightCapture.ts`).

## Taken back to 1967

`src/gull/view/era1967.ts` and the `era: '1967'` option in the scene remove what came later. The removals are by name from OpenStreetMap, or by type:

| Removed | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The marinas (Darthaven, Dart Marina, Noss on Dart) and their berths; the visitor and service pontoons in the river (Deep Water Pontoons, the K and D pontoons, Hoodown, the yacht-club pontoons) | The marinas date from the 1970s onwards. **Unverified**: exact opening years. |
| Supermarkets, the filling station, the leisure centre, the health centre and the park-and-ride at Townstal | Modern out-of-town development. |
| Apartment blocks on the North Embankment (Clarence House, Dartside Court, Kings Quay House, Lee Court, Mayflower Court, Sails, Vavasour House) and Marina View Apartments in Kingswear | Late-20th-century redevelopment. **Unverified**: individual dates. |
| The Flavel arts centre; the toilet block and pumping station by Coronation Park; the community greenhouse | Modern. |
| The Dart lifeboat station | The Dart RNLI station opened in 2007. **Unverified.** |
| The superyacht on the ship buoys, and the marina's lettering on the Noss hall | Modern. |
| The Noss on Dart ferry | It serves the marina. |
| Most of the yachts on the trots (58% removed, and 83% of the motor cruisers) | Far fewer, smaller boats in 1967. |

Kept and believed to suit 1967 (verified below unless marked): the Higher Ferry (floating bridge), the Lower Ferry (tugs and floats), the passenger ferry and its Kingswear landing, the Boat Float, the Embankment, Bayard's Cove and its fort, the Royal Castle Hotel, St Saviour's, the Dartmouth "station", the railway at Kingswear, the river boats' landing stage.

**Remaining approximations in the scenery:**

- **Cars and boats are SailSim's modern shapes.** The cars are fewer (about half) and painted in a 1960s British palette; their shapes are not period. The moored yachts' hulls are modern designs.
- **The trains at Kingswear** are SailSim's. In 1967 the Kingswear line was British Railways; steam had given way to diesel by the mid-1960s. **Unverified**: what SailSim's trains depict versus a 1967 BR diesel multiple unit.
- **Unnamed modern buildings** (later houses and estates) are not removed: OpenStreetMap does not date them.
- **Street furniture, signs and the people walking about** are SailSim's present-day figures.

## Verified history

| Detail | How it appears in the game | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Boat Float is an enclosed tidal mooring with reclaimed land on three sides; the South Embankment (c.1885–1889; sources differ) closes the east side. A road bridge crosses the entrance. | SailSim's Boat Float, at its real position. The crabber *Dorothy May* lies in it: the drop for both operations. | British Listed Buildings 101218121; South West Coast Path *Dartmouth Town Trail* |
| The Royal Castle Hotel was built in 1639 on the then-new quay, re-fronted in the 19th century, and faces the Boat Float. | Its roof is the Bomber Wing's roost in Mission 2. | South West Coast Path trail; Discover Dartmouth |
| Dartmouth "station" on the South Embankment never had trains; it sold rail-and-ferry tickets for Kingswear. | The station building on the Embankment (SailSim's model). Its roof is Mission 1's first landing. | Wikipedia, *Dartmouth railway station*; British Listed Buildings 101297055 |
| The Dartmouth–Kingswear passenger ferry was British Railways' from 1957 to 1972. | SailSim's passenger ferry runs its route. | Wikipedia, *Dartmouth Passenger Ferry*; Simplon Passenger Ship Website |
| The Lower Ferry carries vehicles on floats pushed by tugs, council-run since 1946. | SailSim's Lower Ferry. | Wikipedia, *Dartmouth Lower Ferry*; Simplon *Dart car ferries* page |
| The Higher Ferry is a floating bridge between Sandquay and Noss; vessel No. 7 was built in 1960. | SailSim's Higher Ferry. Sandquay is the submarine's berth in the story. | Wikipedia, *Dartmouth Higher Ferry* |
| Bayard's Cove Fort is an early-16th-century artillery fort at the end of the cove's stone quay. The Old Custom House (4 Bayards Cove) is dated 1739. | The fort's parapet is a perch. The courier's table stands outside a building in the cove row that the game calls the Custom House. **Approximate**: which footprint is No. 4 was not checked. | English Heritage; Historic England list entry 1197571 |
| 3 June 1967 was a Saturday and 7 June 1967 was a Wednesday. | Briefing datelines. | Computed |

**Conflicts noted:** the Embankment is dated 1885 by the walking trails and c.1889 by the listing. One listing says Bayard's Cove quay only reached the fort in 1839; English Heritage dates the quay to the early 16th century. The game shows the quay as it stood by 1967, so neither date matters.

**Time, tide and light.** Mission 1 is set at 13.20 BST on 3 June 1967. The sun's position is computed for that date and place (SailSim's solar model). The tide is a manual curve (high water 13.05, 4.7 m above chart datum), illustrative rather than the real 1967 tide.

## Unverified: check before relying on these

- Pre-decimal currency, in use until February 1971. Not yet used on screen.
- The BBC Home Service became Radio 4 on 30 September 1967. Not yet used.
- St Saviour's has a battlemented west tower; SailSim's data has the church as one block, so the tower is not drawn.

## Invented

- **The occupation is fiction throughout:** the Kommandatura, the parade, the uniforms (simplified Soviet-style khaki with red cap bands and shoulder boards).
- **Characters are all original:** Operative SCRUMPY, Mother, Pte. Borovsky, Lt Voronova, Col. Grushkov, Pte. Kulik, Pte. Osipov, the clerk, the cook, and the Bomber Wing (Gristle, Mrs Wimbush, Old Nelson, The Padre).
- **Boats:** *Dorothy May*, *Brixham Lass* and the submarine *S-197* are fictional.
- **Props** placed by the missions (the courier's table, the barrel and tea mug, the parish pump, the field kitchen, the quay lamp, the reviewing stand) are invented.
- **Cyrillic text**, to be checked by a Russian speaker: "СЛАВА СОВЕТСКОЙ АРМИИ!" (Glory to the Soviet Army!).

## Assets and attribution

No third-party art, sound or font files are included.

- **Gull, people, props and effects** are built in code from three.js primitives (`src/gull/view`).
- **Sound and music** are synthesised in the browser with Web Audio from original note data (`src/gull/app/audio.ts`).
- **Scenery and rendering** come from SailSim (`src/render`, `src/sim`, `src/app`, `public/geo`), vendored as recorded in `VENDORED.md`. SailSim's data carries these attributions:
